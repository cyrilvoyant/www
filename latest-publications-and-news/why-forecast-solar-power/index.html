How forecastable is the sun?

Step of the story · Compute directlyThe approach

When the link is known, compute it: no data and no AI where an explicit model exists. Principle 2: The model at the core.

Cyril Voyant · 18 October 2026

Across 68 Spanish weather stations, our forecastability indicator F retains an inverse relationship with forecast error, examined across ten models and 24 horizons. This result, published in Renewable Energy, supports characterising the signal before comparing algorithms: a large error means something different for a regular sky than for a strongly fluctuating irradiance series.

The study introduces two complementary indicators. The stochastic coefficient of variation, sCV, measures fluctuations relative to a dynamic clear-sky reference. Its theoretical range from zero to one supports interpretation under the framework’s assumptions. F adds information about temporal dependence through autocorrelation. Two series with similar variability can therefore retain different amounts of memory, leaving different amounts of useful information for prediction.

Validation combines one hundred synthetic cyclostationary series with observations from the SIAR network. Simulations allow controlled changes to signal properties; weather stations test the diagnosis against measured conditions. The association between F and forecast errors also appears at individual stations, rather than being solely a consequence of network aggregation. Under extreme synthetic variability, that association can become nonlinear.

For an operator or researcher, the contribution is diagnostic: compare forecasting difficulty, select meaningful baselines and interpret a score in context. It can inform a discussion of forecast risk before choosing a more elaborate model. F is neither a calibrated confidence interval nor a promise of financial savings. Overirradiance events and the quality of the clear-sky reference also require attention. The study measures aspects of inherent forecastability; it does not remove atmospheric uncertainty or establish an operational benefit for every energy system. Those benefits require a separate decision-based evaluation.
