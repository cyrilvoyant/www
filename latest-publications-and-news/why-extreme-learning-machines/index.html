Seven signals, one ELM

Step of the story · Learn frugallyThe approach

When the useful relations are not known well enough, evaluate what the data can teach, with only as much complexity as pays. Principle 3: Complexity only if it pays.

Cyril Voyant · 18 October 2026

At a one-hour horizon, our MIMO-ELM model achieves nRMSE values of 0.179 for solar generation and 0.051 for thermal generation, with R² above 0.98 for both series. Total-demand forecasts remain reliable up to five hours ahead within the study. Published in Applied Energy, these results come from six years of hourly data for the Corsican electricity system.

The model handles seven variables: total demand, solar, wind, hydropower, thermal generation, bioenergy and imports. Its multiple-input multiple-output architecture uses sliding windows and cyclic encodings to represent memory and seasonal variation. The Extreme Learning Machine retains a random hidden layer and fits its output coefficients analytically, avoiding iterative deep-network training.

Evaluation goes beyond an isolated score. The study compares the model with persistence, single-output formulations and an LSTM under a realistic tuning budget. It reports improved skill against the reference methods and small but stable gains over SISO formulations. Wind and bioenergy remain more difficult because of their strong variability. These differences are useful findings in their own right: a shared architecture does not make every energy component equally predictable.

For an energy system, the contribution is a common, rapidly fitted framework for several components. It supports joint investigation of production and consumption. Multiple outputs alone, however, do not impose an exact physical energy balance; enforcing conservation requires an explicit constraint or reconciliation procedure. The reported nRMSE values are not percentage improvements over a competitor. They describe the Corsican evaluation protocol. Transfer to another region should be assessed using that region’s data, constraints and forecast horizons, rather than inferred from architecture alone.
