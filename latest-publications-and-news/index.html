Electrifying transport, heating, cooling and industrial processes is one of the main levers to cut greenhouse-gas emissions and the dependence on fossil fuels, all the more when it goes with a low-carbon electricity mix and sobriety. ELECTRE, a flagship project of the Carnot M.I.N.E.S institute, uses the institute's scientific resources to address the questions this raises, along the whole value chain.

Who runs it. Delphine Gilges-Crampont, Annette Massias and Cyril Voyant (scientific coordinator, Centre O.I.E., Mines Paris – PSL) work on the project as a team of three. Contact: delphine.gilges-crampont@minesparis.psl.eu, annette.massias@minesparis.psl.eu, cyril.voyant@minesparis.psl.eu.

  • Materials for the components of the transition: recycled titanium for catalysis or batteries, composite membranes for electrochemical conversion, conductive biopolymer alloys, laser powder-bed aluminium parts, digital twins of composite structures, lithium-ion batteries and fires in waste-sorting centers.

  • Electrification of processes: hybrid electric, combustion and hydrogen processes.
  • Relevance and limits of electrification: critical materials, environmental foresight including NATECH risks, a shared view of research on electric mobility, and the organization of heat-pump markets.

Haytham El-Houari and Cyril Voyant

The Tunnel Load Simulator is an open-source Streamlit application developed within the SmartTunnel project to generate synthetic electrical load profiles for road tunnels. It combines simplified representations of lighting, ventilation, auxiliary loads, traffic patterns, seasonal effects and stochastic events such as pollution episodes or accidents.

The tool produces high-resolution time series, typically at a 10-minute time step, and uses Monte Carlo simulations to represent uncertainty and operational variability.

The main strength of the simulator is its transparency: the assumptions, equations and code are openly available. It is also reproducible and citable, with a GitHub repository, MIT licence, citation metadata and Zenodo DOI.

The model separates the main physical and operational components of tunnel electricity demand, making the results easier to interpret than a purely black-box approach.

Scientific and practical interest

This tool is not intended to replace measured tunnel data or a calibrated engineering model. Its value is to provide a first-order synthetic generator for prototyping, sensitivity analysis, teaching and preliminary energy-system studies.

It can help explore how tunnel length, traffic, lighting, ventilation, slope, altitude and random events affect electricity demand. It also provides a useful basis for future work on calibration, forecasting, photovoltaic coupling and storage sizing.

Key message

The Tunnel Load Simulator is a transparent, open-source and citable research-software demonstrator for synthetic road-tunnel electricity demand. Its current role is to support early-stage modeling and discussion; with calibration on real tunnel data, it could become a stronger tool for tunnel energy analysis and management.

When Sunlight Stops Being Direct

Measurement and forecasting of the diffuse fraction of solar irradiance using a hybrid stacking model in Saudi Arabia

Diffuse solar radiation remains one of the most complex components of the solar resource, particularly in regions affected by aerosols, humidity, and strong climatic contrasts.

In this study, diffuse irradiance measurements collected across six representative stations in Saudi Arabia were combined with a hybrid stacking framework integrating Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and LightGBM to analyze and forecast the diffuse fraction of solar irradiance.

The work simultaneously explores the physical variability of diffuse radiation and its short-term predictability under desert, coastal, and high-altitude atmospheric conditions.

Results, impact, and perspectives.

Paper accepted in Scientific Report: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-49910-4

Scientific impact: The analysis revealed strong spatial variability in diffuse solar regimes across Saudi Arabia. Coastal stations such as Jeddah reached diffuse fraction values close to 0.95, reflecting intense aerosol and humidity-driven scattering, while inland desert stations remained dominated by direct beam irradiance. The proposed stacking model significantly improved predictive skill compared with standalone learners, with forecast correlations between 0.94 and 0.97, and error reductions reaching 62.48% in some seasonal configurations. These results confirm that diffuse fraction dynamics are strongly climate-dependent and can be effectively learned through hybrid ensemble modeling.

Operational impact: Accurate prediction of diffuse fraction directly affects photovoltaic yield estimation, tilted irradiance transposition, bifacial PV modeling, and solar resource assessment in regions exposed to strong aerosol or humidity variability. The framework offers a practical tool for improving solar forecasting in operational environments where atmospheric conditions evolve rapidly.

Key message: Understanding how the atmosphere redistributes sunlight is not only a physical challenge; it is also a forecasting problem with direct implications for next generation photovoltaic systems .

This work revisits persistence forecasting for periodically varying statistics, including the daily cycle of solar irradiance. BLEND combines the latest observation with an observation aligned to the target phase of the cycle.

The weights are estimated from historical observations in closed form. Phase-wise least squares provides an explicit baseline without iterative optimisation; a moments-based formulation is equivalent in population under its assumptions, but need not give exactly the same finite-sample estimate.

The paper evaluates these operators on controlled simulations and 68 SIAR stations. The separate open-code benchmark v1.1.1 uses held-out synthetic data and compares 21 references, including variants with and without a clear-sky model. It illustrates when phase information helps, without establishing a universal ranking.

Results, impact and resources

Published in Applied Mathematical Modelling, volume 157, article 116988 (2026). Paper DOI · Author version · MATLAB code · Archived code v1.1.1.

Scientific contribution: a phase-aware analytical reference for comparing solar forecasts. The original raw-signal BLEND formulation does not require an external clear-sky model; the extended code also includes clear-sky-based variants, explicitly identified in the benchmark.

Practical use: inspect the assumptions, estimate parameters on past data, and evaluate on unseen observations. The synthetic code results and the measured-data results of the paper are separate experiments.

From Pixels to Tolerances: Rethinking Forecast Validation with the γ-Index

Cyril Voyant

Classical metrics like RMSE evaluate forecasts as if every pixel must match perfectly in space and time. Machines optimize for that. Humans do not think that way.

Read the full post

Cyril Voyant

Classical metrics like RMSE evaluate forecasts as if every pixel must match perfectly in space and time. Machines optimize for that. Humans do not think that way.

This work introduces a tolerance-based framework using the γ-index, where forecasts are evaluated within physically meaningful margins (space, time, intensity). The originality lies in shifting from exact matching to acceptable agreement.

It bridges human operational reasoning and machine evaluation: a forecast slightly shifted but physically coherent is no longer “wrong” : it is usable

Results, impact, and perspectives :

The study demonstrates that classical pixel-wise metrics suffer from a structural limitation known as the double penalty: a small spatial or temporal shift leads to both a miss and a false alarm, artificially inflating the error. In controlled experiments, such shifts can produce large RMSE values (e.g., ~20–40 W/m²), even when the underlying physical structure is preserved.

The γ-index resolves this by embedding tolerances directly into the evaluation. Instead of penalizing displacement, it checks whether the forecast lies within an acceptable region in a joint space–time–intensity domain. The results are clear:

  • Mean γ remains stable (~0.29–0.35), showing robustness to small displacements
  • γ Passing Rate (GPR) reaches ~67–69%, capturing operational usability
  • RMSE, in contrast, reacts strongly to minor misalignments and can misclassify usable forecasts as failures

This changes the interpretation of performance. The question is no longer “Is the forecast exact?” but “Is it operationally acceptable?” For the community, this has direct implications:

  • Energy systems: avoids unnecessary corrective actions (e.g., battery dispatch, backup generation)
  • Meteorology: provides a unified and physically interpretable validation criterion
  • AI/ML: opens the door to tolerance-aware learning, where γ can be embedded as a constraint or loss term

The next step is important: moving from validation to learning. The γ-index is continuous and almost everywhere differentiable, making it compatible with optimization frameworks. This suggests a new paradigm where models are not trained to minimize pixel errors, but to respect physical tolerances.

In short, this parameter is more than a metric. It is a shift in how humans and machines agree on what “good” means

Cyril Voyant

La production scientifique mondiale connaît une croissance sans précédent, amplifiée par l’essor des modèles d’intelligence artificielle générative. Dans ce contexte, la question n’est plus seulement de produire du savoir, mais d’en garantir la visibilité, la robustesse et la réutilisabilité.

Cette page présente un cadre structurant de l’excellence scientifique fondé sur une approche systémique et multiplicative.

La dynamique actuelle se caractérise par :

  • une augmentation rapide du volume de publications
  • une hybridation croissante entre production humaine et assistée par IA
  • une dilution du signal scientifique dans un bruit informationnel massif

Un résultat rigoureux mais non accessible devient structurellement invisible.

Une approche systémique de l’excellence

L’excellence scientifique ne doit plus être considérée comme une propriété intrinsèque, mais comme un système reposant sur plusieurs dimensions interdépendantes.

Fondations

  • Rigueur (R)
  • Transparence (T)
  • Visibilité (V)
  • Adoptabilité (A)

Amplificateurs

  • Collaboration (C)
  • Ouverture (O)
  • Signification sociétale (S)

Formulation du cadre RTVACOS

E∝(R×T×V×A)×(1+C+O+S)

Cette formulation repose sur deux principes :

multiplicativité des fondations → si une dimension est nulle, l’impact global s’annule

effet amplificateur non compensatoire → les dimensions secondaires amplifient uniquement un socle solide

Du SEO au GEO : un changement de paradigme

La visibilité scientifique ne repose plus uniquement sur les citations humaines. Elle dépend désormais de la capacité des systèmes à :

  • identifier des entités (auteurs, méthodes, données)
  • exploiter des métadonnées structurées
  • relier contenus, code et jeux de données

Éléments clés :

  • identifiants persistants (ORCID, DOI)
  • métadonnées explicites
  • dépôts ouverts (code, données)
  • knowledge graphs
  • redondance contrôlée entre plateformes

Un contenu non structuré devient invisible pour les systèmes génératifs.

RTVACOS : un cadre réflexif, non normatif qui n’a pas vocation à produire un score.

Il permet : d’identifier les faiblesses structurelles d’un travail, d’éviter les effets de compensation artificielle, de guider les bonnes pratiques scientifiques. Toute instrumentalisation (optimization superficielle, métriques vides) en annule la portée. Implications opérationnelles et pour les chercheurs :

  • maintenir une identité numérique cohérente
  • publier code et données
  • structurer les métadonnées
  • documenter les processus (y compris échecs)

Pour les institutions :

  • dépasser les indicateurs volumétriques
  • valoriser reproductibilité et réutilisabilité
  • investir dans les compétences data/metadata

Conclusion : La rigueur reste nécessaire mais insuffisante. L’excellence scientifique devient une question d’ingénierie de la connaissance : structurer, relier et diffuser les savoirs pour assurer leur existence effective dans un écosystème algorithmique.

Coherent Multi-Energy Forecasting for Real-Time Grid Management: A Parsimonious MIMO-ELM Approach

Cyril Voyant, Milan Despotovic, Luis Garcia-Gutierrez, Mohammed Asloune, Yves-Marie Saint-Drenan, Jean-Laurent Duchaud, Ghjuvan Antone Faggianelli, Elena Magliaro

Fast, transparent, and operational short-term forecasting of demand and generation across multiple energy sources.

Read the full post

Fast, transparent, and operational short-term forecasting of demand and generation across multiple energy sources.

Modern power systems are becoming structurally more complex. Renewable integration, self-consumption, distributed resources, and partial interconnections introduce strong variability and cross-dependencies between energy sources. In this context, forecasting is no longer about predicting a single time series. It is about predicting a system. A new study published in Applied Energy presents a unified Multi-Input Multi-Output Extreme Learning Machine (MIMO-ELM) framework designed for short-term forecasting of multi-source energy systems. The model simultaneously predicts seven grid variables: total demand and generation from solar PV, wind, hydropower, thermal power, bioenergy, and imports.

What the study shows

Using six years of hourly data from Corsica, the MIMO-ELM framework jointly forecasts seven variables: total demand and generation from solar PV, wind, hydropower, thermal power, bioenergy and imports.

At a one-hour horizon, the published study reports nRMSE values of 0.179 for solar generation and 0.051 for thermal generation, with R² above 0.98 for both series. Total-demand forecasts remain reliable up to five hours ahead within this study. Comparisons with persistence and LSTM must be read with the reported data split, tuning budget and computational constraints.

The multi-output formulation represents several responses in one model. It does not, by itself, impose an exact energy balance. Physical conservation requires an explicit constraint or reconciliation step.

Invisible Research Has No Impact: Engineering Scientific Influence in the AI Era

Cyril Voyant

The research landscape has shifted from scarcity to saturation: producing knowledge is no longer the bottleneck: being discovered, trusted, and reused is.

Read the full post

Cyril Voyant

The research landscape has shifted from scarcity to saturation: producing knowledge is no longer the bottleneck: being discovered, trusted, and reused is.

In an AI-accelerated environment, impact follows attention dynamics and depends on visibility, transparency, and operational adoption rather than venue prestige alone. Work that cannot be found or verified remains effectively nonexistent. Discoverability, metadata quality, reproducibility, and clear failure modes have become integral parts of the scientific method. Rigor builds credibility, transparency builds trust, and usability enables adoption. Visibility is not vanity; it is the infrastructure that allows knowledge to circulate and survive algorithmic filtering. Outreach and collaboration amplify impact only when foundations are solid. In this context, publishing is necessary but insufficient: impact must be engineered. Research that remains invisible delays innovation and contributes little to practice. The objective is no longer to publish more, but to ensure that robust knowledge is discoverable, credible, and usable.

Quality Control Framework for Tilted Solar Irradiance

Mohammed Asloune, Gilles Notton, Cyril Voyant

This work is part of the doctoral research of Mohammed Asloun (University of Corsica & Mines-PSL).

Read the full post

Mohammed Asloun, Gilles Notton, Cyril Voyant (Renewable Energy, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2025.125090).

This work is part of the doctoral research of Mohammed Asloun (University of Corsica & Mines-PSL).

Objectives

This paper introduces a generic and systematic quality control (QC) framework dedicated to tilted solar irradiance time series, a well-known but insufficiently standardized issue in photovoltaic (PV) forecasting and operations. The primary objective is to provide a robust, transferable methodology for detecting and correcting outliers, inconsistencies, and sensor-related anomalies in tilted irradiance measurements. The proposed framework is designed to be site-independent, instrumentation-agnostic, and directly applicable in operational and industrial contexts, with the ambition of serving as a reference standard for PV modelling, forecasting, and performance assessment.

Key results

The proposed QC framework significantly improves the reliability and consistency of tilted irradiance datasets, leading to measurable gains in PV forecasting accuracy, operational robustness, and data bankability, thereby establishing it as a strong candidate for a standard preprocessing step in PV energy systems.

Original publication: Towards Personalized Radio-Iodine Therapy: Modeling, Dynamics, and Clinical Perspectives

A doctoral contribution by Marie Fusella Giuntini, in collaboration with Aix-Marseille, Corsica Universities, Mines-PSL, and la Timone Hospital : Marie Fusella Giuntini, Cyril Voyant, David Taieb and Dominique Barbolosi.

A coherent research line at the interface of modeling and nuclear medicine, two papers proposed with:

- The common objective is to improve the understanding and personalization of radio-iodine therapy (RAI) in metastatic thyroid cancer by combining mechanistic modeling, computational tools, and clinical insight.

- RAI remains essential for differentiated thyroid cancer management, yet response variability across patients is considerable. Personalization requires quantitative frameworks capable of linking uptake, kinetics, and biological response. This study contributes to this emerging field through two complementary publications that structure the methodological and conceptual foundations of patient-specific RAI modeling.

1-Mechanistic Dynamic Surrogate Framework for Personalized RAI Therapy

This article introduces a mechanistic ODE-based model describing individual tumor kinetics under RAI exposure. The model captures uptake, effective clearance, and response dynamics, and is coupled with a surrogate layer allowing rapid simulation of treatment scenarios. The contribution lies in providing a patient-specific predictive tool that can support dose optimization and explore therapeutic trajectories without relying on overly burdensome protocols. It represents a concrete step toward operational personalization.

2-Computational Modeling and Trends in RAI Therapy: A Field with Clinical Promise

The second publication offers a state-of-the-art review of computational approaches for RAI therapy: dosimetric formalisms, PK/PD models, mechanistic methods, AI-based predictions, and hybrid frameworks. It situates current challenges: data scarcity, inter-individual variability, lack of prospective validation; while highlighting the growing role of modeling in moving from empirical dosing toward adaptive, model-informed treatment strategies.

Together, these two papers articulate a clear research trajectory:

-a methodological contribution that demonstrates how individualized dynamics can be formally modeled;

-a conceptual synthesis showing how this work aligns with, and advances, international efforts in computational nuclear medicine

Original publication: Transfer Learning for Wind Speed Forecasting: A Scalable Approach for Data-Scarce Environments

Published in: International Journal of Green Energy (2025)

This paper demonstrates that transfer learning can accurately forecast wind speed in data-scarce regions by reusing models trained on data-rich sites. Across 4,830 experiments, TL achieved accuracy comparable to direct learning (ΔnRMSE < 0.01). The approach offers a scalable, low-data solution for expanding wind forecasting to under-instrumented areas.

Why This Matters

Wind energy forecasting is essential for grid stability, but many regions lack long-term, high-quality wind data.

Conventional machine-learning models require extensive local measurements, making them impractical for data-scarce environments.

This study explores transfer learning (TL) as a way to overcome that limitation, reusing models trained in data-rich locations to forecast wind speed in poorly instrumented areas.

Key Results

  • Dataset: 70 meteorological stations across Spain, covering various climatic zones.
  • Methods compared: Transfer Learning (TL), Direct Learning (DL) and Two model families: Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) and Autoregressive (AR).
  • Forecast horizons: 30 min → 6 h (360 min).
  • Metrics: normalized RMSE (nRMSE) and normalized MAE (nMAE).
  • Findings: TL performance nearly matches DL: nRMSE ≈ 0.297 (TL) vs 0.292 (DL) at 30 min horizon, nRMSE ≈ 0.674 (TL) vs 0.640 (DL) at 6 h horizon

Abstract

This work presents a robust framework for quantifying solar irradiance variability and forecastability through the Stochastic Coefficient of Variation (sCV) and the Forecastability (F). Traditional metrics, such as the standard deviation, fail to isolate stochastic fluctuations from deterministic trends in solar irradiance. By considering clear-sky irradiance as a dynamic upper bound of measurement, sCV provides a normalized, dimensionless measure of variability that theoretically ranges from 0 to 1. F extends sCV by integrating temporal dependencies via maximum autocorrelation, thus linking sCV with F. The proposed methodology is validated using synthetic cyclostationary time series and experimental data from 68 meteorological stations (in Spain). Our comparative analyses demonstrate that sCV and F proficiently encapsulate multi-scale fluctuations, while addressing significant limitations inherent in traditional metrics. This comprehensive framework enables a refined quantification of solar forecast uncertainty, supporting improved decision-making in flexibility procurement and operational strategies. By assessing variability and forecastability across multiple time scales, it enhances real-time monitoring capabilities and informs adaptive energy management approaches, such as dynamic outage management and risk-adjusted capacity allocation

Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments, 2025, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.seta.2025.104588

Abstract

Accurate solar energy output prediction is fundamental to integrating renewable energy sources into electrical grids, maintaining system stability, and enabling effective energy management. However, conventional error metrics, such as Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Skill Scores (SS), fail to capture the multidimensional complexity of solar irradiance forecasting. These metrics lack sensitivity to forecastability, rely on arbitrary baselines (e.g., clear-sky models), and are poorly adapted to operational needs.

To address these limitations, this study introduces the NICE^k metrics (Normalized Informed Comparison of Errors, with k = 1, 2, 3, Σ), a novel evaluation framework offering a robust, interpretable, and multidimensional assessment of forecasting models. Each NICE^k score corresponds to a specific L^k norm: NICE^1 emphasizes average errors, NICE^2 highlights large deviations, NICE^3 focuses on outliers, and NICE^Σ combines all three dimensions.

The methodology combines synthetic Monte Carlo simulations with real-world data from the Spanish SIAR network, encompassing 68 meteorological stations in diverse climatic regions. Forecasting models evaluated include autoregressive approaches, Extreme Learning Machines, and smart persistence. Results show that theoretical and empirical NICE^k values converge only when strong statistical assumptions are met (e.g., R² ≈ 1.0 for NICE^2). Most importantly, the composite metric NICE^Σ consistently outperforms conventional metrics in discriminating between models (e.g., p-values < 0.05 for NICE^Σ vs > 0.05 for nRMSE or nMAE).

Across increasing forecast horizons, NICE^Σ yields consistently significant p-values (from 10⁻⁶ to 0.004), while nRMSE and nMAE often fail to reach statistical significance. Furthermore, traditional metrics (nRMSE, nMAE, nMBE, R²) cannot reliably distinguish between models in head-to-head comparisons. In contrast, the NICE^k family demonstrates superior statistical discrimination (p < 0.001), broader variance distributions, and better inter-study comparability.

This study confirms the theoretical and empirical validity of the NICE^k framework and highlights its operational relevance. It establishes NICE^k as a robust, unified, and interpretable alternative to conventional metrics for evaluating deterministic solar forecasting models.

Abstract

Clearsky models are widely used in solar energy for many applications such as quality control, resource assessment, satellite-base irradiance estimation and forecasting. Deterministic and probabilistic results are compared to traditional benchmarks, including ARMA with McClear-generated Clearsky data and quantile regression for probabilistic forecasts. ELM matches or outperforms these methods, providing accurate predictions and robust uncertainty quantification. This approach offers a simple, efficient solution for real-time solar forecasting. By overcoming the stationarization process limitations based on usual multiplicative scheme Clearsky models, it provides a flexible and reliable framework for modern energy systems.

I've experienced this troubling phenomenon firsthand: an article I had little hope for exploded with hundreds of citations, while another piece I considered my best work remained in obscurity. After twenty years of research, one thing is clear: in a rapidly evolving academic ecosystem, publishing alone is no longer sufficient.

A Saturated Ecosystem

The explosion has been brutal. In the international journals I work with, submissions have surged by over 30% in recent years. Rejection rates now reach 70% in some disciplines. Even more striking: the geography of research has been radically transformed. Where the United States dominated fifteen years ago, China now represents over 40% of global submissions in many fields.

This quantitative explosion masks deeper transformations. The emergence of AI-assisted writing tools facilitates large-scale manuscript production. Some countries have implemented financial incentives that can reach $43,000 for a publication in Nature or Science - peaks observed in China between 2008 and 2016.

The result? Between 10% and 30% of scientific articles remain uncited several years after publication, depending on the discipline. This isn't always a quality issue: it's often a discoverability problem. In this saturated environment, visibility has become a scientific skill in its own right.

Before Submission: Preparing for Discoverability

Visibility work begins well before submission. A title of 8-15 words, precise without jargon, an explicit abstract, and keywords covering the terminological variants of your field significantly improve findability via Google Scholar. Search engines first index the metadata you provide them.

Journal selection deserves strategic reflection that's often overlooked. The question isn't "which journal is most prestigious?" but "where does my audience actually read?" Publishing in a highly ranked journal but in a field distant from yours may flatter the ego, but sometimes has less impact than a second-tier journal that's central to your niche.

Metrics like CiteScore (measuring average citations over 4 years) or the "Cites per document" indicator on SCImago help choose a journal actually read by your peers. Better to publish "in the conversation" than "beside the conversation."

Open Access: A Global Opportunity

The open access movement has created unprecedented opportunities for research visibility. Repository platforms like arXiv (multidisciplinary), medRxiv (health), or EarthArXiv (Earth sciences) accelerate the circulation of ideas. In many fields, a preprint signals scientific openness, generates early feedback, and can initiate citations before formal publication.

For researchers in Europe, national agreements often allow avoiding open access publication fees. Simply linking your institutional email and ORCID identifier can automatically provide these benefits.

This "green" strategy ensures worldwide free availability of your work. Concretely: even if your article is published in a journal with a $3,000 annual subscription, anyone in the world can freely access your repository version after the embargo period.

Writing to Be Discovered

A principle I now apply: one idea equals one article, adaptable according to disciplines. Multi-subject manuscripts get lost in the crowd. I prefer short formats for targeted results - they're often more read and cited than very dense articles.

It's about doing "academic SEO": aligning title, abstract, keywords, and subheadings with your audience's typical queries. Make your figures self-sufficient with explanatory captions and clear licenses (like Creative Commons) to encourage reuse.

Systematically deposit your datasets and scripts following FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable). A Data Management Plan from the beginning of a project facilitates this approach. A GitHub repository with DOI via Zenodo increases reuse and mentions, making you discoverable by completely different audiences.

For licenses, favor Creative Commons (CC-BY for example) which allows reuse with attribution. Open access isn't limited to "Green" (repository deposit) or "Gold" (direct paid publication): the emerging "Diamond" model offers free access for everyone.

After Publication: Orchestrating Dissemination

Publication day isn't the end of the story, it's the beginning of your research's public life. I now apply a reproducible plan:

  • Immediate: Deposit the accepted version in institutional repository with ORCID synchronization. This step takes 10 minutes and ensures permanent archiving.
  • Professional: Targeted announcement on LinkedIn in 3-4 sentences explaining the question your article answers, a key result, and why it matters. This is where you'll reach decision-makers and industry leaders who can transform your results into action.
  • General public: A short popularization post on Medium or a blog explaining the "why" and potential uses. Some research deserves to influence public debate.
  • Academic: Sharing on ResearchGate and Academia.edu while respecting publisher policies.
  • Necessary vigilance: Only massively disseminate quality work - otherwise we contribute to the information noise we denounce.

Structural Solutions Needed

Beyond individual strategies, reforms are necessary. Researchers excel in fundamental research (Nobel prizes and Fields medals attest to this). But they are increasingly absent from editorial boards of major applied journals, particularly in strategic fields. Structural solutions could include:

  • Revising valorization policies by placing quality at the center, with minimum thresholds over 2 years rather than a quantity race
  • Recruiting dedicated administrative staff to free researchers from management tasks
  • Recognizing editorial functions in career evaluation
  • Training in "publication literacy" from the PhD level

In a saturated landscape where we must navigate between global quantitative explosion and quality maintenance, visibility becomes an impact multiplier. An article in a top journal is good. An article that people find, read, use, and build upon for their own research is infinitely better.

Visibility isn't academic vanity: it's the necessary condition for years of research to find their social, economic, and scientific utility.
