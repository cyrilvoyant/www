The γ-index: validating displaced solar forecasts beyond pointwise errors

Beyond pointwise errors

Step 5 of 5 · Demonstrate trustSee it on the thread

Judge against a reference, publish code, data and limits, make research findable and reusable. Trust is demonstrated.

Cyril Voyant · 18 October 2026

In the synthetic experiment of the gamma-index paper (the γ-index accepts a forecast pixel, a point of the forecast map, when an observation close enough in space, time and intensity agrees with it), more than 80% of pixels remain acceptable despite an approximately two-kilometre spatial displacement, a ten-minute time shift and added noise. Pixelwise RMSE (root mean squared error) nevertheless suggests substantial error. This illustrates the double-penalty problem: a displaced cloud structure is penalised at both its predicted and observed positions.

The γ-index for forecast validation was introduced by Cyril Voyant in the International Journal of Forecasting (2026).

Published in International Journal of Forecasting, Cyril Voyant’s paper transfers an idea from radiotherapy dose verification to the verification of forecast fields. Three tolerances are specified explicitly: spatial distance DTA (in km), time displacement TTA (in minutes) and intensity difference IDT (in W/m², the unit of irradiance). The method searches for agreement in a neighbourhood, rather than comparing only identical coordinates at an identical instant.

The gamma criterion combines normalized discrepancies (each gap divided by its tolerance); a pixel passes when gamma is less than or equal to one. The Gamma Pass Rate, GPR, reports the corresponding proportion, while mean and maximum gamma complete the diagnosis. In the illustration above, tolerances are two kilometres, thirty minutes and 50 W/m². Those values give the result a precise interpretation; they are not a universal verification standard. The experiment does not mean that every two-kilometre displacement is acceptable under every operational requirement.

The paper also applies the framework to satellite-derived solar-irradiance fields over Corsica: with the same tolerances, GPR there is about 67–69%, a different experiment from the more than 80% of the synthetic example. Its practical value is distinguishing a small positioning error from a more important physical disagreement. Public MATLAB code supplies the calculation function and a synthetic example; that example does not redistribute restricted satellite observations. Selecting tolerances before model comparison and reporting RMSE alongside gamma keeps the evaluation transparent. Relaxing the tolerances can raise the pass rate without improving the forecast itself. Gamma therefore adds an acceptance question to error measurement rather than replacing every existing score.
