When Sunlight Stops Being Direct

Measurement and forecasting of the diffuse fraction of solar irradiance using a hybrid stacking model in Saudi Arabia

Haifa Harrouch, Nadjem Bailek, Bilel Zerouali, Ilyes Abidi, Hanene Guesmi, Cyril Voyant, Jihad A. Younis, Maher Jebali & Rabab Triki

Diffuse solar radiation remains one of the most complex components of the solar resource, particularly in regions affected by aerosols, humidity, and strong climatic contrasts.

In this study, diffuse irradiance measurements collected across six representative stations in Saudi Arabia were combined with a hybrid stacking framework integrating Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and LightGBM to analyze and forecast the diffuse fraction of solar irradiance.

The work simultaneously explores the physical variability of diffuse radiation and its short-term predictability under desert, coastal, and high-altitude atmospheric conditions.

Results, impact, and perspectives.

Paper accepted in Scientific Report: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-49910-4

Scientific impact: The analysis revealed strong spatial variability in diffuse solar regimes across Saudi Arabia. Coastal stations such as Jeddah reached diffuse fraction values close to 0.95, reflecting intense aerosol and humidity-driven scattering, while inland desert stations remained dominated by direct beam irradiance. The proposed stacking model significantly improved predictive skill compared with standalone learners, with forecast correlations between 0.94 and 0.97, and error reductions reaching 62.48% in some seasonal configurations. These results confirm that diffuse fraction dynamics are strongly climate-dependent and can be effectively learned through hybrid ensemble modeling.

Operational impact: Accurate prediction of diffuse fraction directly affects photovoltaic yield estimation, tilted irradiance transposition, bifacial PV modeling, and solar resource assessment in regions exposed to strong aerosol or humidity variability. The framework offers a practical tool for improving solar forecasting in operational environments where atmospheric conditions evolve rapidly.

Key message: Understanding how the atmosphere redistributes sunlight is not only a physical challenge; it is also a forecasting problem with direct implications for next generation photovoltaic systems .
