Research and projects

From solar variability to useful forecasts

My research at Mines Paris–PSL / O.I.E. examines what can be predicted, which information a model needs, and how to evaluate a forecast for its intended use. The work connects statistical physics, time series, machine learning and energy systems, with a continuing strand in medical physics.

Solar variability and forecastability

A strong daily cycle can conceal the variability caused by clouds. The stochastic coefficient of variation (sCV) separates these effects; the forecastability index F studies how this variability relates to prediction error. The published evaluation includes 68 Spanish stations.

Read the sCV and forecastability story · Renewable Energy paper

Parsimonious forecasting

I study how signal structure and model complexity affect forecast quality. This includes autoregressive models, Extreme Learning Machines, clear-sky preprocessing and cyclostationary persistence. BLEND uses periodically correlated structure to improve a persistence reference without training a forecasting model; the separate MIMO-ELM study forecasts seven energy signals jointly.

BLEND explained · MIMO-ELM results and limits · ELM implementation guide

Evaluation that matches the question

NICE metrics measure errors relative to a persistence baseline across several error norms. The γ-index evaluates agreement within explicit spatial, temporal and intensity tolerances. Both approaches complement conventional metrics; their interpretation depends on the baseline, sampling and tolerances.

NICE explained · The γ-index explained · Methods and public code

Energy systems and medical physics

Forecasts support the operation of photovoltaic systems, storage and microgrids. My project work connects prediction to energy management and control, particularly in island systems. In medical physics, my interests include radiobiological dose equivalence, radiotherapy modeling and radioiodine kinetics.

Research programs and collaborations

I contribute to collaborative programs in forecasting, renewable integration and energy management. The portfolio below separates current programs from earlier collaborations. Official project records are linked where available.

Current programs

Fine4Cast · ANR / PEPR TASE · 2023–2028
Forecasting renewable production and consumption from minutes to days at fine geographic resolution. Programme overview.

ELECTRE · Mines Carnot · since 2025
Scientific coordination of the components addressing AI for energy, forecasting and multi-source systems.

Mines Paris–PSL / University of Corsica / CNRS agreement · 2024–2027
Intermittent solar forecasting and energy-management algorithms for smart microgrids.

Earlier programs

Sensor-augmented weather prediction at high resolution, combining observations and numerical modeling. ANR project record (initial duration: 48 months).
