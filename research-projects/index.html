Research and projects

My research at the Observation, Impacts, Energy center of Mines Paris – PSL, in Sophia Antipolis. center of Mines Paris – PSL asks three questions: what is predictable, how much model is needed, and how to judge a forecast for its intended use. It relies on collective programs, shown here with their dates and partners.

In brief

  • Three strands. Solar variability and forecastability (sCV, F); parsimonious forecasting (persistence, BLEND, ELM); evaluation that matches the question (NICE, γ-index).
  • Applications. Island energy systems, storage and microgrids; in medical physics, dose equivalence in radiotherapy.
  • Programs. Three current ones (Fine4Cast, scientific coordination of Flagship project of the Carnot M.I.N.E.S institute on the electrification of uses (2023-2026): materials, storage, industrial processes, relevance and limits of electrification., an agreement with the University of Corsica and the Center national de la recherche scientifique, the main French public research organization.) and four completed since 2015, on a timeline by domain.
  • Checkable evidence. Each strand links to the published paper, the story that explains it and the open code.

Solar variability and forecastability

A strong daily cycle can hide the variability caused by clouds. The stochastic coefficient of variation (sCV) separates the two: it measures cloud-driven variability once the daily cycle is set aside. The forecastability index F relates this variability to the forecast error one can expect. The published evaluation covers 68 Spanish stations.

The sCV and forecastability story · Which forecast for which signal? · method and MATLAB code · Renewable Energy paper

Parsimonious forecasting

I study how the structure of a signal and the complexity of a model shape forecast quality: autoregressive models (the forecast is a weighted sum of past values), Extreme Learning Machines (ELM: one-hidden-layer neural networks whose only computed weights are the output weights), clear-sky preprocessing (the theoretical irradiance without clouds) and cyclostationary persistence. Persistence, the reference forecast, repeats the last observed value; a cyclostationary series has statistical properties that repeat periodically, here every day. BLEND uses this structure to improve on persistence without training a model; the separate MIMO-ELM study (multiple inputs, multiple outputs) forecasts seven energy signals jointly.

BLEND explained · BLEND method and code · MIMO-ELM results and limits · ELM implementation guide

Evaluation that matches the question

NICE metrics divide a model's errors by those of persistence, for several error norms. The γ-index (gamma) judges agreement between forecast and observation within explicit tolerances in space, time and intensity. Both complement the usual metrics; how to read them depends on the baseline, the sampling and the tolerances.

Energy systems and medical physics

Forecasts are used to run photovoltaic systems, storage and microgrids (small local electricity networks). Our projects connect forecasting to energy management and control, especially in island systems. In medical physics, I work on radiobiological dose equivalence, radiotherapy modeling and radioiodine kinetics.
