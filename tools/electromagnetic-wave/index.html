Electromagnetic wave: electric and magnetic fields

At 50 MHz (50 million oscillations per second), the wave profile advances by about 6 m every 20 ns (nanoseconds), while at each point the electric field E and the magnetic field B only oscillate in place. You separate what oscillates from what travels.

A course experiment whose values are computed within the stated model and are not research findings: The chapter and its slides (French) · All experiments · Glossary.

Try it and interpret

Advance by one eighth of a period (the duration of one full oscillation, 20 ns here) and follow a fixed point: it oscillates without leaving. Then start the slow-motion playback: the profile does move forward. Double the frequency: the wavelength (the distance between two crests) is halved.

How does a wave travel while its fields oscillate transversely?

The fields E and B oscillate in place, in phase, perpendicular to the direction of travel. Each point repeats the same oscillation with a delay proportional to distance: this delay is what moves the wave profile forward, at speed c in vacuum.

E(x,t)=E0cos(ωt−kx)

B=Ec

λ=cν

I=ε0cE022

Definitions

E, B
The electric (in V/m) and magnetic (in teslas; the tool shows B in nanoteslas, nT) fields, perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel. E₀ is the amplitude of E.
x, t
Position along the direction of travel, and time.
ω, k
Angular frequency (2π × frequency) and wave number (2π divided by wavelength).
ν, λ
Frequency and wavelength.
c, ε₀
The speed of light and the electric permittivity of vacuum.
I
Mean intensity: the power transported per unit area, in W/m².

Assumptions and limitations

Plane wave, linear polarization, in vacuum. B is scaled to E to compare shapes; its physical values are shown separately. The animation is slowed down: two seconds per period.
