Are my data FAIR? A self-assessment in 20 questions

Where should you start to make your research data FAIR? Answer 20 questions in about three minutes to identify what to improve first in your metadata, access, formats and licenses. The checklist gives you a score out of 20 across the four FAIR principles. Data should be findable, accessible, interoperable (readable by other software) and reusable.

Who is it for? PhD students, researchers, engineers and data stewards preparing a deposit, a data management plan (DMP, the document that describes how the data of a project will be produced, described, stored and shared) or a publication. Is my project FAIR enough? An example of a 20-point self-assessment

This is only an example: a teaching grid proposed here, not a standard or a certification. The questions follow the GO FAIR principles (F1 to R1.3) plus a few practical criteria; the scoring and thresholds are choices of this page. For a reference assessment: the RDA FAIR Data Maturity Model (Research Data Alliance) or the automated tool F-UJI. Acronyms underlined with dots open an explanation on click or tap.

Five questions per letter: first the FAIR sub-principles, in their official order, then practical questions (tagged “Practice”); each "yes" is worth 1 point, "partly" 0.5. Total out of 20. Benchmarks: below 7: needs work; 7 to under 14: good; 14 and above: very good. The tool names the axes to work on and the limiting factor: the weakest letter, which bounds real reuse.

Mapping drawn up by the O.I.E. center: each open component covers part of the principles; together they cover them all. Simplified yes/no reading: a component may serve a principle only in part. For Toolbox and S3P2, R refers to reusing the processes through their WPS interfaces; check the license and reuse conditions of the data separately.

Suggested citations depend on the component and the request. Webservice-Energy is registered with DataCite and adds DOIs on request. The ABC-Rad DOI resolves to the citation page listed in the GeoNetwork row.

Why this grid?

This is only a teaching example, not a standard or a certification. The twenty questions follow the FAIR principles published by Wilkinson et al. (2016) and promoted by the GO FAIR initiative (F1 to F4, A1 and A2, I1 to I3, R1.1 to R1.3), plus a few practical criteria. The scoring (1 point for “yes”, 0.5 for “partly”, 0 otherwise) and the thresholds (5 and 10 out of 20) are choices made for this page: they help you learn what makes data reusable, not rank projects.

For a reference assessment, use:

  • the RDA FAIR Data Maturity Model (Research Data Alliance, the international organization that develops recommendations for sharing research data): shared indicators and their priority levels;
  • F-UJI, an automated tool that assesses a published dataset from its persistent identifier, for example a DOI (Digital Object Identifier, an identifier that stays valid even if the web address changes).
