Heat engine and Carnot limit: cycle and energy balance

Even with no losses at all, an engine between 400 °C and 20 °C turns at most 56.5% of the heat received into work (this is the Carnot limit). You see why this limit, set by the second law of thermodynamics, depends only on the two absolute temperatures (in kelvins: °C + 273.15).

A course experiment whose values are computed within the stated model and are not research findings: The chapter and its slides (French) · All experiments · Glossary: Carnot efficiency · French version of this experiment.

Questions about the Carnot cycle

What is the maximum efficiency of a Carnot engine?

Between 400 °C and 20 °C, the reversible limit is 56.5%. It is calculated as η = 1 − T_cold / T_hot, with both temperatures in kelvins.

Why must the temperatures be converted to kelvins?

The formula uses a ratio of absolute temperatures. Using 400 and 20 directly would give 95%. The correct ratio uses 673.15 K and 293.15 K.

Why does efficiency stay below 100%?

Reaching 100% would require a cold reservoir at 0 K. Between 400 °C and 20 °C, even a reversible engine rejects 435.5 J to the cold reservoir for every 1,000 J it receives.

Try it and interpret

Between 400 °C and 20 °C, for 1000 J received, the limit allows about 564.5 J of work. Lower the cold reservoir or raise the hot one: the limit rises, without ever reaching 100%. Then follow the four stages on the temperature–entropy diagram (entropy S, in J/K: in a reversible exchange, its change is the heat exchanged divided by the absolute temperature).

Why must a heat engine reject part of the heat it receives?

This is the second law: over a cycle, an engine cannot convert all the heat received from a single reservoir into work. The minimum rejection to the cold reservoir sets the Carnot limit, which depends only on the two absolute temperatures.

ηCarnot=1−TcoldThot

W=ηQhot

Qrejected=Qhot−W

Absolute temperatures, in kelvins.

Definitions

η
Efficiency: work W divided by heat received Qhot. ηCarnot is its limit.
W
The work delivered per cycle.
Qhot, Qrejected
The heat received from the hot reservoir and the heat returned to the cold reservoir, per cycle.
Thot, Tcold
The temperatures of the hot and cold reservoirs, in kelvins.
T–S diagram
Temperature T against entropy S. For the ideal cycle, the area of the rectangle is the maximum work.
Reversible
An ideal cycle, with no friction and no temperature gap during exchanges: the only one to reach the limit.

Assumptions and limitations

Cyclic engine between two reservoirs. The T–S rectangle represents the ideal cycle, not that of a less efficient machine: its area gives the maximum work.
