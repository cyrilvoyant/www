Chi-square test: observed counts, expected counts and contributions

Over 120 throws of a die, a single face appearing 68 times instead of 20 contributes 115.2 of the 138.5 points of χ² (chi-square: the overall measure of the gap between observed and expected counts). You learn to look for where the departure lies, instead of stopping at the p-value (the probability of a gap at least this large if the die were fair).

A course experiment whose values are computed within the stated model and are not research findings: The chapter: summary, formulas and FAQ (slides in French) · All experiments · Glossary: chi-square test.

The χ² on qualitative data, step by step

The three course examples, with the χ² law drawn: the red zone is where H₀ is rejected at the 5% risk. Click a cell, move the slider and see from which count the result becomes significant.

Show the χ² formulas

χ2=∑cells(O−E)2E

goodness of fit: E=Nk,df=k−1

independence: Eij=LiCjN,df=(ℓ−1)(c−1)

O is the observed count in a cell, E the count expected under H₀, N the total count, k the number of cells (fit to equal proportions), Lᵢ and Cⱼ the totals of row i and column j, ℓ and c the numbers of rows and columns. Course rule: if the computed χ² exceeds the table χ² (3.84 for 1 df, 5.99 for 2, 9.49 for 4, 16.92 for 9 at the 5% risk), H₀ is rejected. Expected counts must be large enough (in practice E ≥ 5).

Reading the p-value. State a null hypothesis H₀ and an alternative H₁. Under H₀, the p-value p is the probability of a test statistic at least as extreme as the one observed. Set the threshold α in advance, often at 5%, then reject H₀ when p < α.

What does the α threshold control?

Under H₀ and the test assumptions, α limits the false rejection rate over repeated experiments. Interpret a significant result alongside the effect size and its uncertainty.

Exercises

Taken from the course and its tutorials (no exam paper).

  1. Random number table (course). Among 250 numbers, the unit digits 0 to 9 appear 17, 31, 29, 18, 14, 20, 35, 30, 20 and 36 times. Is the table suspicious?
    Show the answer

    E = 25 per cell; χ² = 23.28 with 9 df; table χ² = 16.92. 23.28 > 16.92: H₀ rejected, p = 0.0056. The table is suspicious.

  2. Serum and healing (course). Group A (serum): 75 healed out of 100; group B (no serum): 65 out of 100. Is the serum useful?
    Show the answer

    Expected counts 70 and 30 in each group; χ² = 2.38 with 1 df, below 3.84; p = 0.12. H₀ cannot be rejected: the serum is not significantly effective. With the slider, group A would need at least 78 healed out of 100 (the rest unchanged).

  3. Stomach cancer and sex (course). Men: 53 pylorus, 66 stomach body, 75 cardia; women: 48, 33, 23. Is the location linked to sex?
    Show the answer

    Expected counts about 66, 65, 64 (men) and 35, 34, 34 (women); χ² = 12.83 with 2 df, above 5.99; p = 0.0016. H₀ rejected: the location is linked to sex.

Try it and interpret

For 120 throws of a fair die, expect 20 occurrences of each face. Change one face and inspect its contribution (its share of the χ²). The 2×2 table uses the course case 75/25 and 65/35: p ≈ 0.1228 without continuity correction (an adjustment for small counts), so no rejection at the 5% level (the starting hypothesis is rejected only if p < 0.05).

Which cells contribute to the discrepancy between observations and the hypothesis?

Each cell contributes (O−E)²/E, and χ² is their sum. Reading these contributions shows where the departure lies, which the p-value does not say. For an equal difference, a cell with a small expected count weighs more.

Show the formulas

χ2=∑(O−E)2E

Fair die: E = N/6, 5 degrees of freedom. Independence in a 2×2 table: E = (row total × column total)/N, 1 degree of freedom.

Where: O is the observed count in a cell, E the count expected if the tested hypothesis holds, N the total count and Σ the sum over all cells.

Definitions

O, E
The observed count and the count expected under the tested hypothesis.
N
The total count.
Σ
The sum over all cells.
χ²
The test statistic: the sum of the contributions (O−E)²/E.
Degrees of freedom
The parameter of the reference distribution: 5 for the die, 1 for the 2×2 table.
p-value
The probability of exceeding the χ² obtained if the hypothesis is true, under the approximate distribution. A small p-value says the departure is hardly compatible with the hypothesis, not that it is large: see the course chapter Testing a difference and reading its uncertainty.

Assumptions and limitations

Independent observations, mutually exclusive categories, actual counts. The p-value is approximate: with small expected counts, the automatic conclusion is suspended. Failing to reject demonstrates neither fairness nor independence.
