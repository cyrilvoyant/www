Forecast photovoltaic power: directly, or through irradiance?

Over one year of open measurements at Risø (Denmark), at a 1-hour horizon, forecasting the photovoltaic (PV) power directly or first forecasting the irradiance received by the panels (GTI) and then converting it gives almost the same error: nRMSE 0.47 against 0.48. Starting from the horizontal irradiance (GHI) adds one step, the transposition, and the error rises to 0.53. nRMSE is the root mean squared error divided by the mean output; here with an autoregression, over daytime hours. Each conversion adds its own error, and errors add up in squares. You will see where the final error comes from, what the size of the plant changes, and what happens when the grid asks the plant to limit its power.

Try it: the three routes over one year

When the grid limits the power

The grid operator may impose a power limit, announced in advance or not. Choose the limit, the announcement, and how PV direct treats its inputs. The coloured band marks the curtailment; the solid line marks its start.

What the data show (AR, 1 h)

  • Announced shutdown, PV direct in kW: after the curtailment, its inputs keep zeros for 24 h, and it forecasts too low: 1.01 kW RMSE after the curtailment, against 0.64 kW for GTI then PV.
  • PV direct divided by the available power: during a shutdown, this is a division by zero; 271 of 351 forecasts become impossible after the curtailment.
  • Tested proposal (divide by the announced available power and mask the zero hours): no more division by zero, and PV direct returns to the level of the other routes (0.64 kW after the curtailment). But with a 60% limit, the same division inflates the values that did not reach the limit: 1.35 kW during the curtailment, against 0.54 kW without division.
  • Irradiance routes: their inputs do not see the curtailment; they adjust through the peak power and the announced set-point.
  • Not announced: only PV direct sees the drop in its last value; it is the best route during the curtailment (2.39 kW, against 5.06 kW for GTI then PV).
Theory: how the error propagates and adds up

Notation: P the measured power, P̂ its forecast, Gh the GHI, Gt the GTI, T the transposition (GHI → GTI), C the conversion (GTI → PV). The final error is e=P^−P. Route GHI then GTI then PV:

P^=C(T(G^h))

Adding and removing the same quantities, the final error becomes exactly the sum of three stage errors, all in kW of PV: e₁ comes from the GHI forecast, e₂ from the transposition, e₃ from the conversion.

e=C(T(G^h))−C(T(Gh))⏟e1+C(T(Gh))−C(Gt)⏟e2+C(Gt)−P⏟e3

On average over the year (the brackets ⟨ ⟩ denote a mean), the final squared error is the sum of the squared errors of the stages, plus cross terms:

⟨e2⟩=⟨e12⟩+⟨e22⟩+⟨e32⟩+2(⟨e1e2⟩+⟨e1e3⟩+⟨e2e3⟩)

If the stage errors are uncorrelated, the cross terms vanish and the RMSEs add up "in squares": the largest dominates, and a small stage weighs little.

RMSE≈RMSE12+RMSE22+RMSE32

The GTI then PV route only has e₁ (GTI forecast) and e₃; PV direct has a single stage. A forecast error goes through the following conversions, which multiply it by their slope (C′, T′: the change of the output for a small change of the input):

e1≈C′(Gt)T′(Gh)(G^h−Gh)

C′ is about 7.5 kW per kW/m². T′ becomes large when the sun is low and the panels steeply tilted: this is why the GHI route suffers in winter. The disc on the page shows the share si of each stage, and the text under the disc gives ρ, the weight of the cross terms:

si=⟨ei2⟩∑j⟨ej2⟩,ρ=⟨e2⟩−∑j⟨ej2⟩⟨e2⟩

Example (AR, 1 h, peak power unchanged): stage RMSEs 0.869, 0.416 and 0.194 kW; final RMSE 0.919 kW; ρ = -0.14. The negative sign means that the forecast and transposition errors partly cancel.

The error measures, with epers the error of persistence of P at the same horizon:

nRMSE=⟨e2⟩⟨P⟩,NICEk=(⟨|e|k⟩)1/k(⟨|epers|k⟩)1/k,k=1,2,3

Change of peak power: PV direct can work on the relative power x, then return to kW.

x(t)=P(t)Pc,P^(t+h)=Pcx^(t+h)

Curtailment, with s the allowed share of Pc and A the available power: a forecast that knows the announcement is capped at A; the variant "divided by the available power" replaces Pc by A in the division, which divides by 0 when s = 0.

Protocol and limits

Data. SOLETE, version 3 (D. V. Pombo, Technical University of Denmark, DTU), CC BY 4.0 licence: GHI, GTI and the power of a 10 kW PV plant measured at the same place, Risø (55.69° N, 12.10° E). The 5-minute file is averaged here to 30-minute steps, in UTC. The solar-elevation column of the file is one hour off; the sun position is recomputed (pvlib).

Frozen training. Every model is trained once, from 1 June to 31 August 2018, then evaluated without retraining from 1 September 2018 to 31 August 2019, at daytime (sun above the horizon). Forecasts are set to 0 at night and bounded below by 0.

Models. Those of the published PV_ELM_pred code, imported without change: persistence, BLEND, least-squares autoregression, ridge-regularised ELM (200 neurons, 8 draws, temporal cross-validation). Inputs: 48 past values (24 h), standardised with the training statistics, and the sun position at the target time (sine of elevation and of azimuth). PV_ELM_pred uses the hour and the day of the year; with a single training summer, those features make the models forecast summer days in winter: the sun position, known in advance, carries both the hour and the season.

Live computation. The forecasts are computed in Python and rounded to 10 W; the page recomputes all the errors in your browser. These errors were compared with the Python computation on 17,552 values: largest relative difference below 10⁻¹³.

Limits. One plant, one climate, one training summer; a simple transposition and conversion, fitted on the same data. The conversion ignores module temperature. Curtailments simulated on 8 sunny days of summer 2019, at least 4 days apart. This is a teaching calculation on open data, not the result of an article.

The words of the page

PV, P
photovoltaic; P is the electric (AC) power produced by the plant, in kilowatts (kW).
Pc
the peak power: the nominal power of the plant, 10 kW here. The capacity scenario halves or doubles it.
GHI
Global Horizontal Irradiance: the solar irradiance received by a horizontal surface, in watts per square metre (W/m²). It is the quantity most often measured and forecast.
GTI
Global Tilted Irradiance: the irradiance received in the tilted plane of the panels, in W/m². This is what the panels convert into electricity.
Transposition
the calculation from GHI to GTI. The direct and diffuse components are first separated (Erbs model), the direct beam is capped at 1.1 times its clear-sky value, then projected on the plane of the panels (Hay and Davies model). The tilt (53°) and the orientation (198°, slightly west of south) are not published: they are fitted here on the training summer.
Conversion
the step from GTI to power: P = a·G + b·G², with G the GTI in kW/m², a = 7.478 kW per kW/m² and b = -1.030, fitted on the training summer, then bounded between 0 and Pc.
Horizon
the time between the last known measurement and the forecast time, here from 30 minutes to 6 hours, in 30-minute steps.
Persistence
copy the last known value. It is the minimal reference.
BLEND
a mix of persistence and of the value of the day before at the same time, with one weight per half hour of the day.
AR
autoregression: a weighted sum of the last 48 values (24 h) and of the sun position at the target time, with weights computed by least squares.
ELM
Extreme Learning Machine: a neural network with one hidden layer drawn at random (200 neurons), of which only the output weights are computed.
RMSE, nRMSE
RMSE is the root mean squared error, in kW; it weighs large errors more. nRMSE divides it by the mean measured output.
NICE¹, NICE², NICE³, NICEΣ
the error divided by that of persistence of the PV power, at the same times: NICE¹ for the mean absolute error, NICE² for RMSE, NICE³ for the cubic error, NICEΣ for their mean. Between 0 and 1, the forecast beats persistence; above, the page shows "> 1".
Curtailment
a power limit imposed on the plant (by the grid operator, for example), here from 10:00 to 14:00 UTC (Coordinated Universal Time, 12:00 to 16:00 Danish summer time).
Available power
the power the plant is allowed to produce: Pc normally, the announced limit during curtailment.
