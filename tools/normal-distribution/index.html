Interactive normal distribution: area, probability and z-score

In any normal distribution (the bell curve), about 95% of values lie within 1.96 standard deviations (the typical spread) of the mean. You go from a value to a probability, and from a probability to a threshold, without a table, thanks to the z-score: the number of standard deviations between a value and the mean.

A course experiment whose values are computed within the stated model and are not research findings: The chapter: summary, formulas and FAQ (slides in French) · All experiments · Glossary: normal distribution.

From the normal law to tests

  • The z test: placing a mean on this same curve to compare it with a reference value.
  • The Jarque-Bera test: checking whether data are compatible with a normal law (skewness and kurtosis).

Try it and interpret

At z = 1.96 the area to the left (the probability of a smaller value) is about 0.975. Read the central zone from −1.96 to +1.96: about 95%. Also try the inverse calculation, from probability to threshold.

How do you convert a value into a probability, or find a threshold from an area?

Convert x into a z-score, the number of standard deviations separating it from the mean. The area under the curve to the left of z is the probability sought. The inverse calculation starts from an area and finds the threshold.

Show the formulas

z=x−μσ

Φ(z)=P(Z≤z)

With Z ∼ N(0, 1), the normal distribution with mean 0 and standard deviation 1. σ is the standard deviation.

Where: x is a value, μ the mean, σ the standard deviation, z the z-score and Φ(z) the probability P that a variable Z of law N(0, 1) stays below z (the area left of z).

Definitions

x, μ, σ
A value, the mean and the standard deviation.
z
The z-score: the number of standard deviations between x and the mean.
Z, N(0, 1)
A standard normal variable: mean 0 and standard deviation 1.
Φ(z), P
Φ(z) is the probability P that Z stays below z: the area to the left of z.

Assumptions and limitations

The calculation assumes the selected normal distribution; it does not show that measurements are normal. A population interval is not a confidence interval for a mean.
