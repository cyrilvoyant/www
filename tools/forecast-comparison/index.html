Compare forecasts: when is a more complex model worth it?

At a 30-minute horizon, a neural network does no better than a simple autoregression (a forecast from past values): RMSE (the typical error, in kilowatts, kW) is 1.43 kW for both, against 1.65 kW for persistence (copying the last value). At 6 hours, both divide the error of persistence by about 2.5, and the network only gains about 4% more. These five forecasts concern the same photovoltaic installation in Palaiseau and the same times. You will learn to judge a model by what it gains on a simple reference, not by its complexity.

The words of the comparison

Persistence
copy the last observed value: the minimal reference of any forecast.
Cyclic persistence
copy the value observed 24 hours before the target time. It follows the daily cycle but ignores the current weather.
BLEND
convex combination of persistence and cyclic persistence: two positive weights adding up to 1, one set of weights per half hour of the day, computed on the first half of the data.
AR
autoregression: a weighted sum of the values of the last 24 hours and of the time of day and of year, with weights computed by least squares.
ELM
Extreme Learning Machine: a neural network with one intermediate layer drawn at random. Only the output weights are computed, by regularized least squares.
Horizon
the time between the last known value and the value to forecast, here from 30 minutes to 6 hours.
RMSE, nRMSE, nMAE
RMSE is the root mean squared error, in kW; it penalizes large errors. nRMSE and nMAE divide RMSE and the mean absolute error by the mean output of the times counted.
NICE¹, NICE², NICE³
the error of the model divided by that of raw simple persistence (last observation, without a night mask), at the same horizon: NICE¹ for the mean absolute error, NICE² for RMSE, NICE³ for the cube root of the mean cubed absolute error, which weighs large errors even more. Between 0 and 1: the forecast is useful. Above, it is worse than persistence: the page then shows "> 1".
Daytime / day and night
"daytime" counts only the times when the sun is up. Forecasts are set to zero at night. Counting the night changes the values: the scope must accompany the results.

Protocol: public Palaiseau data (AC power, 30-minute steps, first two years, maximum output 16.5 kW); weights computed on the first half, evaluation on the second. The published PV_ELM_pred code provides the calculations; the scaling of past values is computed here on the first half only. A single ELM setup (200 neurons, 8 draws), a single installation: this is neither a universal ranking nor the result of an article.
