Interactive ROC curve: sensitivity, specificity and prevalence

At 10% prevalence (10% of the population has the disease), a test with 78.8% sensitivity (it detects 78.8% of those with the disease) and 84.1% specificity (it clears 84.1% of those without) gives only 35.6% of people with the disease among positive results (constructed example). You understand what a positive result means, and what depends on the population.

A course experiment whose values are computed within the stated model and are not research findings: The chapter: summary, formulas and FAQ (slides in French) · All experiments · Glossary: ROC curve · French version of this experiment.

Try it and interpret

Change the threshold (the result above which the test is positive): the point moves along the curve. Then change prevalence alone: sensitivity, specificity and the curve do not move, as expected in this model. Predictive values (the probability of having the disease given a positive result, or of not having it given a negative one) do change. Finally increase the separation (the gap between typical results of people with and without the disease): the curve moves away from the diagonal.

What changes with a test threshold, and why do predictive values depend on prevalence?

Lowering the threshold gains sensitivity and loses specificity: the ROC curve shows this trade-off. These two quantities describe the test in people with and without the disease. Predictive value answers another question, that of the person with a positive result, and therefore depends on the proportion of people with the disease.

Se=TPTP+FN

Sp=TNTN+FP

PPV=Se·pSe·p+(1−Sp)(1−p)

p is the prevalence.

Definitions

ROC curve
Sensitivity against the false-positive rate 1 − Sp as the threshold varies (Receiver Operating Characteristic).
TP, TN, FP, FN
True positives, true negatives, false positives and false negatives.
Se, Sp
Sensitivity (people with the disease who test positive) and specificity (people without it who test negative).
p
Prevalence: the proportion of people with the disease in the population.
PPV, NPV
Positive and negative predictive values: the probability of having the disease if the test is positive, of not having it if negative.
AUC
The area under the ROC curve. It summarizes the test without choosing a threshold.

Assumptions and limitations

Two normal distributions with the same standard deviation: an illustration, not a clinical evaluation. AUC gives neither the best threshold nor an individual probability of disease.
