Interactive t-test: means, variability and p-values

Is a difference between means large relative to its uncertainty?

An experiment from Cyril Voyant’s course, running in your browser. Full course and slides (French) · Choose another experiment.

Reading the quantities. Δ is the difference between the means; n₁ and n₂ are the group sizes; s₁ and s₂ their standard deviations. sₚ is the estimated common standard deviation and ν the degrees of freedom. t expresses the mean difference in standard-error units. The p-value is the probability, assuming equal means and the test model, of a result at least as extreme; it is not the probability that the hypothesis is true.

The relationship to understand

t=Δsp1n1+1n2

sp2=(n1−1)s12+(n2−1)s22n1+n2−2

ν=n1+n2−2

Student's t-test with pooled variance.

Try it and interpret

Keep the difference and variability fixed, then increase the sample size: the p-value changes while the difference between means stays the same. With a zero difference, t=0 and p=1.

Assumptions and limitations

This interface illustrates a two-sided pooled-variance test for two independent groups of equal size, at the 5% level. Check normality and equal variances. Paired measurements require a different framework. Displayed points are constructed from the summaries, not real observations.
