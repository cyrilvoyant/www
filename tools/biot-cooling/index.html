Food cooling: Biot number and core temperature

At Bi = 1 (the Biot number, unitless: the resistance of the food’s interior to heat compared with that of its surface), the uniform-temperature model announces 116 min to close 90% of the gap with the surrounding medium; the core of the food needs 164 min. You know when one temperature is enough, and when it misleads.

A course experiment whose values are computed within the stated model and are not research findings: The chapter and its slides (French) · All experiments · Glossary: Biot number.

Try it and interpret

With h = 25 W/m²/K (surface heat exchange), Lc = 0.02 m (characteristic length: volume divided by surface) and λ = 0.5 W/m/K (thermal conductivity of the food), Bi = 1: internal gradients matter. Increase λ or reduce Lc: Bi falls and the curves come closer. The uniform model does not depend on λ: its curve therefore does not move when λ changes.

Where do the 116 minutes come from? The example assumes a water-rich food: density ρ = 1,050 kg/m³ (the mass of one cubic meter) and specific heat capacity cp = 3.6 kJ/kg/K (the energy needed to warm 1 kg by 1 °C). The time constant is τ = ρ · cp · Lc / h = 1,050 × 3,600 × 0.02 / 25 = 3,024 s, that is 50 min: after that time, 37% of the temperature gap remains. For only 10% to remain, you must wait τ × ln 10 = 2.3 × 3,024 s ≈ 6,960 s, that is 116 min. The core of the food needs 164 min: at Bi = 1, the single-temperature model therefore announces the end of cooling 48 min too early. This is the same formula as τ = m · cp / (h · A) written below, because m / A = ρ · Lc.

When can an entire food product be represented by a single temperature?

When Bi is small (usual guide: Bi < 0.1), heat moves quickly inside the food compared with what the surface removes: one temperature is enough. As Bi grows, the core lags behind the surface and the two must be told apart.

Bi=hLcλ

T(t)=T∞+(Ti−T∞)e−t/τ

τ=mcphA

T(t): uniform-temperature model, valid when Bi is small.

Definitions

Bi
The Biot number: internal conduction resistance compared with surface convection resistance.
h
The surface convection coefficient, in W/(m²·K).
Lc
The characteristic length: volume divided by heat-transfer area.
λ
The thermal conductivity of the food, in W/(m·K).
T(t), Ti, T∞
Temperature at time t, the initial temperature and that of the surrounding medium.
τ, m, cp, A
The time constant of the uniform model, mass, specific heat capacity and heat-transfer area.

Assumptions and limitations

Bi < 0.1 is a guide. The core curve uses the first conduction term for a slab, valid after the earliest instants; properties and convection are constant, with no phase change. This does not validate a food-safety process.
