Compton scattering: angle and energy sharing

A 255.5 keV photon (kiloelectronvolts, a unit of energy) keeps 66.7% of its energy after scattering at 90° (a collision with an electron that deflects it by 90°), and only half at 180° (sent straight back). You read, angle by angle, how the energy is shared between photon and electron.

A course experiment whose values are computed within the stated model and are not research findings: The chapter and its slides (French) · All experiments · Glossary: Compton effect · French version of this experiment.

Try it and interpret

Start from 255.5 keV at 180°: the photon keeps 127.75 keV and the electron receives 127.75 keV. Return to 0°: the transfer vanishes. Then change the incident energy at a fixed angle: the electron’s share changes too.

How does the scattering angle change the energy shared by a photon and an electron?

The photon gives the electron a share of its energy, zero at 0° and largest at 180°. At a given angle, this share increases with the photon energy. Total energy is conserved: E′ + K = E₀.

E′=E01+E0511(1−cosθ)

K=E0−E′

Energies in keV; 511 keV is the rest energy of the electron.

Definitions

E₀, E′
The photon energy before and after scattering.
K
The kinetic energy received by the electron.
θ
The angle between the photon directions before and after scattering.
511 keV
The electron rest energy, mₑc².
keV
The kiloelectronvolt, a unit of energy: 1 keV = 1,000 eV.

Assumptions and limitations

Free electron at rest. The diagram gives directions and energies, neither collision probabilities nor dose.
