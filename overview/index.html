At a glance: research, software, courses and tools

The public work of Cyril Voyant in medical physics and energy forecasting, courses and software included, each item linked to its source: paper, DOI (permanent identifier), repository, team. Free to reuse, crediting the authors. The short version, in 30 seconds; the thread connecting this work.

In brief

  • One way of working in five steps: compute when the link is known, complete the model when it falls short, learn frugally when a relation is missing, then make the tool usable and demonstrate that it works.
  • Three fields: energy, medical physics and research practice, carried out with the co-authors named on each page.
  • Everything is public: 15 software packages with DOIs, 5 courses under the CC BY 4.0 license (free reuse, crediting the authors) and tools to try in the browser.

Map of the work

Main ideas

Understand before you computeStart from the human question, model the system, then choose the tool.Beat a simple referenceEvery forecast is first compared with persistence (the last value copied); a model that does not beat it adds nothing.Complexity must payA frugal model (ELM: neural network with random input weights; autoregression: weighted sum of past values) first; heavier only if the gain is shown.Trust is demonstratedPublic code, data, licenses and verification; AI is a tool, declared.

Research lines

  • Solar and energy forecastingPersistence (copy the last known value), BLEND (a mix of persistence and the value one cycle earlier), ELM and the Corsican electricity mix (a frugal neural model whose output weights alone are fitted), dimensionality reduction (keep only the part of the input that serves the forecast).
  • Evaluating forecastsNICE (error divided by that of persistence: useful between 0 and 1), γ-index (tolerates small shifts in space, time and intensity), sCV and forecastability (cloud-driven variability, computed before any model), prediction intervals (a range meant to contain the measurement).
  • Medical physicsBiologically equivalent doses (LQL-Equiv) (LQL: linear-quadratic-linear model): compare two radiotherapy schedules on one scale; radioiodine therapy of the thyroid: a three-equation model.
  • AI and languageLanguage models as interfaces to validated codes.
  • Assessing researchInfluence index (a log-based measure of an institution's influence), the RTVACOS framework (seven criteria, from rigor to societal relevance), scientific publishing.

Words used on this page

DOI
Permanent identifier of a paper or archived software: the link stays valid even if the website changes.
Persistance / persistence
The forecast copies the last known value; it is the reference to beat.
BLEND, CLIPER
Two mixes of simple references: persistence with the value one cycle earlier (BLEND), or with the mean (CLIPER).
ELM
Extreme learning machine: a one-hidden-layer neural network with random input weights; only the output weights are computed.
MIMO
Multiple inputs, multiple outputs: one model forecasts several signals.
NICE
A forecast's error divided by that of persistence; between 0 and 1, the forecast adds something.
sCV, F
Cloud-driven variability and the forecastability of a signal, computed before any model.
Indice γ / γ-index
A test that accepts small shifts in space, time and intensity.
LQ, LQL
Linear-quadratic and linear-quadratic-linear models of the effect of a radiation dose on tissue.
BNCT
Boron neutron capture therapy.
PV, EDF
Photovoltaic; Électricité de France, the French utility.
MATLAB
A numerical computing language and environment.
CC BY 4.0
Creative Commons Attribution license: free reuse, provided the authors are credited.
AI Act
The European Union regulation on artificial intelligence.
RTVACOS
Seven criteria: rigor, transparency, visibility, adoption, collaboration, outreach, societal relevance.
ROC
ROC (receiver operating characteristic) curve: a test's sensitivity against its false-positive rate.
PASS, UE, B.U.T.
The French first-year health program; teaching unit; French bachelor of technology.
