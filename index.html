Cyril Voyant

Understand before you compute.

I build models to inform decisions in energy and medicine, helping an engineer size a system, an operator anticipate production, a physicist interpret a dose, and tools that make them usable. I start from the problem: what is known about the system, the data available and the precision really needed. I use an explicit model when it fits, complete it with learning when a relation is missing, and keep extra complexity only when its benefit is demonstrated. AI is fully part of the toolbox; evaluation and responsibility stay human.

One method, four complementary routes

The domain changes; the method does not. The route is chosen by the problem and the decision to inform, not by the tool; extra complexity is kept only when its benefit is demonstrated.

Compute directly

When the link is known, compute it with an explicit model, calibrated and validated on data; learning is added only for a demonstrated benefit.

Compute, don't learn: radiobiological dose equivalence · Simulate what you cannot yet measure: road tunnels · How forecastable is the sun?

Hybridize

When the model falls short, complete it: keep the model, learn only what is missing.

Thyroid cancer and radioiodine: model first · Better persistence, without training a neural network

Learn frugally

When the useful relations are not known well enough, evaluate what the data can teach, with only as much complexity as pays.

Seven signals, one ELM · Satellite forecasting: one cell or the whole field? · Frugal models for solar forecasting (in French)

AI in its place

Language models help people use validated tools; the calculation, the checking and the responsibility stay with validated codes and people.

Let the language model read, let the validated code compute

Demonstrate trust

Judge against a reference, publish code, data and limits, make research findable and reusable.

About

Cyril Voyant is a physicist and Research Director at Mines Paris – PSL (Centre O.I.E.). Combining medical physics in radiotherapy (since 2003) and energy-forecasting research (PhD in 2011), he works on solar and energy forecasting, forecast evaluation, frugal AI and language-model interfaces to scientific codes.
