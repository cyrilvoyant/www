Teaching

Five courses, open to all under CC BY 4.0, from health studies to energy. The same habit runs through them: start from the signal or the data, compare with a simple reference, measure what a model adds.

Open courses

Most courses are in French; the English guide to the open courses describes each one, chapter by chapter.

Forecasting the intermittent solar resource

Mines Paris – PSL engineering curriculum, applied research project UE14 · 59 slides · English and French · CC BY 4.0

How to forecast solar irradiance one hour ahead, on ten years of real measurements from Ajaccio, adding complexity only where it pays.

  • The solar signal: geometry, clear sky and the clear-sky index (an additive deviation), what can and cannot be predicted.
  • The data: ten years of hourly measurements, quality control, day and night.
  • The references to beat: simple persistence, smart persistence, climatology.
  • Autoregressive models fitted by least squares, the model order, the sliding window.
  • Extreme Learning Machines: principle, number of neurons, the risk of overfitting.
  • Evaluation: nRMSE, nMAE, skill, NICE; test on the year 2007, daytime and day and night.
  • Online learning and frugality: how much model is really needed?

Biostatistics for health studies (in French)

First-year health studies (PASS, UE8) · 3 chapters, 246 slides · in French · CC BY 4.0

The statistical tools a future health professional needs to read a study, interpret a test and decide with data.

  • Why statistics in health: variability, therapeutic adjustment, correlation and causation, hidden variables.
  • Probabilities: random experiments, events, counting, conditional probabilities, the Bayesian approach.
  • Diagnostic tests: contingency tables, sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, ROC curves.
  • Descriptive statistics: measurement scales, graphs, central tendency, dispersion, skewness.
  • The normal distribution: standardization, the z table, intervals.
  • Hypothesis tests: null and alternative hypotheses, risks, comparison of means.
  • Pearson correlation, linear regression, coefficient of determination and significance.
  • χ² goodness-of-fit and independence tests, with exercises.

Physics for health studies: electromagnetic waves, X-rays and imaging (in French)

First-year health studies (PASS-PACES), physical bases of medical imaging · 110-slide lecture and 2 corrected exercise sessions · in French · CC BY 4.0

The physical bases of medical imaging, from the wave to the CT scanner.

  • The nature of electromagnetic waves: speed, sinusoidal wave, wave vector.
  • Wave-particle duality: photon energy E = hν, the electronvolt.
  • The electromagnetic spectrum and its energy ranges.
  • The energy levels of the atom and the case of hydrogen.
  • Absorption, emission, fluorescence, Auger effect, spectroscopy; an application: pulse oximetry.
  • X-rays: the Coolidge tube, bremsstrahlung and fluorescence radiation, spectrum, radiation protection.
  • Photon-matter interactions: photoelectric effect, Compton effect, pair production, attenuation and half-value layer.
  • Imaging: contrast, conventional radiography, CT scanning and windowing.
  • Two exercise sessions corrected step by step (18 exercises).

Industrial physics: thermodynamics, water vapor and refrigeration (in French)

B.U.T. biological engineering, food science and biotechnology, 2nd year · 5 chapters, 139 slides and handouts · in French · CC BY 4.0

The thermodynamics of a food-industry technician, up to how a refrigeration machine works.

  • Quantities: pressure, temperature, work and heat; sensible and latent heat.
  • Changes of state: evaporation, boiling, triple point, condensation.
  • Ideal and real gases; enthalpy and entropy.
  • Clapeyron, entropy and Mollier diagrams; reading the P-h diagram of R134a; linear interpolation.
  • Water vapor: expansion and compression, altitude, condensation, saturation vapor pressure, relative humidity.
  • First and second laws, heat engines, engine cycles and steam cycles.
  • The four-step refrigeration cycle on the Mollier diagram, its COP, industrial refrigeration.
  • Refrigerants and their regulation (CFC, HCFC).
  • Five chapters of written handouts come with the slides.

Heat transfer for food engineering (in French)

B.U.T. biological engineering, food science and biotechnology, 1st year · 24 slides · in French · CC BY 4.0

How heat moves through a food, and what it takes to cool or freeze it.

  • The three modes of exchange: conduction, convection, radiation.
  • Basic concepts: isothermal surfaces, heat flow and heat flux density.
  • Conduction: Fourier's law, conduction thermal resistance.
  • Free and forced convection: heat transfer coefficient h, convection resistance.
  • The Biot number: comparing internal and surface resistances.
  • Thermal properties of fresh and frozen foods: heat capacity, initial freezing temperature.
  • The refrigeration power of cooling and freezing, the amount of water to freeze.
  • Energy balance: core temperature (conduction) and surface temperature (convection) over time.

Teaching and research supervision

I supervise and co-supervise doctoral research on solar forecasting, satellite imagery, parsimonious models, energy-system control and radioiodine modeling. Previous doctoral work includes solar forecasting, air quality and solar-potential estimation.

At Mines Paris–PSL, my teaching includes solar resource forecasting and academic tutoring of international internships and gap years (UE45). Earlier teaching spans biostatistics, thermodynamics, biophysics and numerical modeling at the University of Corsica.
