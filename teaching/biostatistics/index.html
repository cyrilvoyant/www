Biostatistics for health studies: the course in three chapters

A test with a sensitivity (the share of sick people detected) and a specificity (the share of healthy people cleared) of 99% can be wrong about ten times out of eleven when it is positive, if the disease is rare: this course gives the statistics a future clinician needs to read a study, interpret a test and decide with data. Probability, diagnostic tests, descriptive statistics, the normal distribution, hypothesis tests, correlation, regression and χ².

Start: chapter 1 →

What the course contains

First-year health studies (PASS, UE8) · 3 chapters, 246 slides in French · summaries, experiments and FAQ in English · CC BY 4.0

Goal
Read a study, interpret a diagnostic test and choose a simple statistical test.
Prerequisites
High-school mathematics: fractions, powers, reading graphs.
Suggested path
Chapter 1 (probability, Bayes, ROC curve with the interactive tool), chapter 2 (normal distribution with the interactive table, tests), chapter 3 (correlation, regression, chi-squared). Each chapter: the 10-point summary, the slides, then the FAQ.
  • Why statistics in health: variability, therapeutic adjustment (adapting the dose to each patient), correlation and causation, hidden variables (unmeasured factors that create an apparent link).
  • Probabilities: random experiments, events, counting, conditional probabilities (the probability of A given B), the Bayesian approach (revising a probability in the light of a result).
  • Diagnostic tests: contingency tables (the counts of two variables crossed), sensitivity, specificity, predictive values (the probability of being sick, or not, given the test result), ROC curves (sensitivity against 1 − specificity as the test threshold varies).
  • Descriptive statistics: measurement scales, graphs, central tendency (mean, median), dispersion (standard deviation), skewness (the shape of the distribution).
  • The normal distribution (the bell curve): standardization (bringing the mean to 0 and the standard deviation to 1), the z table (the probabilities of the standard normal distribution), intervals.
  • Hypothesis tests: null (no effect) and alternative hypotheses, risks of error, comparison of means.
  • Pearson correlation (r, between −1 and 1: the strength of the linear link), linear regression (the line that predicts y from x), coefficient of determination (R², the share of variation explained) and significance.
  • χ² (chi-squared, which compares observed counts with expected counts) goodness-of-fit and independence tests, with exercises.

Try it: ROC curve · Normal distribution · Student t-test · Influential point in regression · Significance and R² · χ² test · Regression intervals

The words of the course
PASS, UE8
The French first year of health studies; UE8 is its teaching unit 8.
Sensitivity, specificity
The share of sick people whose test is positive; the share of healthy people whose test is negative.
Positive predictive value
The probability of being sick when the test is positive. It depends on prevalence, the share of sick people.
ROC curve
Sensitivity against 1 − specificity as the test threshold varies.
p-value
The probability, if the null hypothesis is true, of a result at least as extreme as the one observed. A small one flags a result poorly compatible with the null hypothesis (H0).
Hidden variable
An unmeasured factor that can create an apparent link between two variables.
χ²
A statistic comparing observed counts with expected counts.

Part of the course comes from the work of Dominique Barbolosi, mathematician, Aix-Marseille Université.

In brief

  • A biostatistics course in three chapters and 246 slides, for the first year of health studies (PASS, teaching unit UE8). The chapters are read here in English; the slides are in French.
  • It goes from probability and Bayesian reasoning to diagnostic tests (sensitivity, specificity, ROC curves), descriptive statistics, the normal distribution and hypothesis tests, then correlation, linear regression and χ² tests, with medical examples.
  • Course by Cyril Voyant, trained as a medical physicist, under CC BY 4.0: each chapter has a ten-point summary with the formulas, experiments to manipulate, a FAQ, and a link to its slides and PDF in French.
