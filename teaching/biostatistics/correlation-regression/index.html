Chapter 3: correlation, linear regression and χ² tests

Covariance and Pearson correlation, the regression line and R², the significance of r, the χ² goodness-of-fit and independence tests, with exercises.

Slides (69, in French) · PDF in French (5.1 MB) · Questions

The chapter in 10 points

Key point. With 1000 pairs, r (the correlation coefficient, between −1 and 1) = 0.1 can be significant (the zero-correlation hypothesis is rejected at the chosen level) while explaining only 1% of the variation: significant does not mean useful.

  1. The scatter plot shows the shape of the link; covariance (how two variables vary together; Σ is the sum over the n measured pairs, xᵢ and yᵢ their values, x̄ and ȳ the means of the two series) cov(x, y) = Σ(xi − x̄)(yi − ȳ)/(n − 1).
  2. Pearson correlation: r = cov(x, y)/(sx sy), where sx and sy are the standard deviations, between −1 and 1, to be interpreted with care (non-linear link, outliers, causation).
  3. Least-squares line: b1 = cov(x, y)/sx², b0 = ȳ − b1 x̄, slope b1 and intercept b0; it is used to predict y from x.
  4. In simple least-squares linear regression with an intercept, R² = r² measures the share of the variation of y explained in the sample; this equality does not carry over to every model.
  5. Under H0: ρ = 0 (the null hypothesis: no correlation), where ρ is the correlation in the population, for independent pairs from a bivariate normal distribution, t = r√(n − 2)/√(1 − r²) follows a Student distribution with n − 2 df (degrees of freedom: the number of free values in the calculation). A non-linear link can escape Pearson's r.
  6. Residuals (the gaps between the observed values and the line), tests on the slope and on the intercept, and the 95% prediction interval around a predicted value.
  7. Qualitative variables: χ² = Σ (O − E)²/E, where O are the observed counts and E the expected counts.
  8. χ² goodness of fit: k − 1 degrees of freedom (df) for k probabilities fixed in advance; estimating m parameters can bring the df down to k − 1 − m under the conditions of the test. Example from the course: 52.75 exceeds 9.488 for 4 df at the 5% level.
  9. χ² test of independence on a contingency table, with (R − 1)(C − 1) degrees of freedom for R rows and C columns: treatment and recovery, nationality and brand, sex and site of a cancer.
  10. The synthesis exercises chain comparison of means, correlation, regression line and prediction interval on data from smokers.

Going further: questions and answers

What is the difference between a confidence interval and a prediction interval?

In simple linear regression with independent normal errors of constant variance: 95% CI of the mean = ŷ0 ± t(n−2; 0.975) se √[1/n + (x0−x̄)²/Sxx]. For a future observation, add 1 under the root. Here Sxx = Σ(xi−x̄)² and se² = Σresidual²/(n−2). The prediction interval therefore keeps the individual scatter; 1.96 is only an asymptotic approximation of the quantile.

Is a significant r enough to make a prediction useful?

No: with 1000 pairs, r = 0.1 can be significant while explaining only 1% of the variation in a simple regression with an intercept. Rejecting ρ = 0 under a model proves neither causation nor predictive usefulness; the latter is also measured on data not used to fit the model.

Why can a single outlier change everything?

Least squares weigh the square of the deviations: a point far away in x can strongly change the fitted slope and correlation, increasing or decreasing the apparent relationship depending on its position. Always look at the scatter plot and the residuals before concluding.

What to do when the expected counts of a χ² are small?

The asymptotic approximation can be poor; examine the size and structure of the table. A justified grouping or an exact method may be suitable, notably Fisher's test for a 2×2 table in its setting. Yates' continuity correction does not guarantee to solve the small-count problem.
