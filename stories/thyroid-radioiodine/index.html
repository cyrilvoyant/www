Thyroid cancer and radioiodine: model first, learn only what is missing

Step 2 of 5 · HybridizeSee it on the thread

When the model falls short, complete it: keep the model, learn only what is missing. The model at the core.

How many sessions of radioactive iodine, how far apart, and at what activity? An intelligible model of three equations explores these questions before any patient is treated. Learning will be added only where the model is not enough.

Marie Fusella Giuntini and Dominique Barbolosi, with Cyril Voyant and David Taieb

In brief

  • According to the article, which analyses retrospective data, the model can indicate whether a patient responds after two or three sessions and three measurements of thyroglobulin (a protein measured in the blood), instead of the conventional seven or eight sessions.
  • Three equations (radioactive iodine activity, tumour cells, thyroglobulin) describe how metastatic thyroid cancer responds to radioiodine therapy; their parameters were calibrated on 50 patients.
  • The tumour doubling time (how long the tumour takes to double in size without treatment) separates the profiles: about 9.8 months for a refractory profile (one that responds poorly to radioactive iodine) and 66.6 months for a responding one (it responds well), in the simulator's published examples.
  • The model makes it possible to explore the number of sessions, the interval between them and the activity before treating. Learning is planned only where the model falls short, in a hybrid approach that keeps the model at its core.
  • It is research and teaching work: not a medical device, and no clinical recommendation.

The finding

When a disease can be described by a model we can read term by term, that model comes first: it explains, it can be calibrated on real patients, and it lets one test protocols by simulation (in silico). Artificial intelligence is not needed to do what the model already does well. The model and its simulator, RAIR-Sim, are the work of Marie Fusella Giuntini and Dominique Barbolosi, with Cyril Voyant and David Taieb (Scientific Reports, 2026). A student can follow each equation below, then change the protocol and watch thyroglobulin respond.

The question

Metastatic differentiated thyroid cancer is often treated with repeated sessions of radioactive iodine. Some tumours respond; others become refractory, meaning they no longer respond adequately to radioiodine. Thyroglobulin (Tg) is a protein measured in the blood and used here to follow the tumour burden. Which protocol, and for which patient profile?

The theory, in plain words

Three quantities evolve over the months:

  • the activity of the administered iodine, the rate of radioactive decays, decreases over time, and each session adds new activity to what remains;
  • the tumour cells grow at a rate set by their doubling time Td (the time needed to double in the model without treatment), and are killed in proportion to the activity present;
  • thyroglobulin is produced by the tumour cells and cleared by the body.

The equations are solved session by session, so a whole protocol can be simulated in seconds. A short doubling time means a fast-growing tumour: that is what separates a refractory profile from a responding one.

Where it sits on the complexity ladder

Second level: simulation from a model we understand. The next step is a hybrid digital twin, a simulation specific to each patient and updated at each session: keep the model, and let a learning method estimate only what the model does not capture. One candidate is a frugal extreme learning machine (ELM), a neural model whose hidden weights are drawn at random and whose output weights alone are fitted. This is mathematics- and medicine-informed learning, not a black box replacing the model. No learning added where the model meets the need; learning, in hybrid form, where it helps.

The example in figures

With the parameters of the simulator's public examples, 3.7 GBq (gigabecquerels, the unit of administered activity) per session every 3 months; thyroglobulin concentrations are given in ng/mL (nanograms per millilitre):

  • Refractory profile (Td = 9.8 months): thyroglobulin reaches about 1,386 ng/mL at 79 months after one session, and stays near 12 ng/mL after eight.
  • Responding profile (Td = 66.6 months): about 14 ng/mL after one session, and 0.13 ng/mL after eight.

These are model outputs for two typical profiles, not predictions for a patient.

Comparing protocols by simulation, not deciding treatment

  • Brings: a readable way to compare protocols by simulation; one parameter, the doubling time, that separates profiles; open code that anyone can run (MATLAB).
  • Limits: two typical profiles and parameters calibrated on a population; it is not a clinical decision tool and needs prospective validation. The learning part of the hybrid twin is work in progress, separate from the published results.
