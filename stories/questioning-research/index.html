Questioning how we do research: influence, integrity, excellence

Step 5 of 5 · Demonstrate trustSee it on the thread

Judge against a reference, publish code, data and limits, make research findable and reusable. Trust is demonstrated.

How is research judged, published, shared and reused, and how could it be done better? This page is a starting point: each card asks one question and leads to the page that answers it in detail, whether an interactive tool, an essay or a guide. The approach is the same as everywhere else on this site: an explicit reference, several complementary measures, and stated limits.

The question. How should an institution, a funder or a researcher read research performance at a time when output keeps growing and AI writing tools accelerate it further? A volume indicator rewards size; a single score hides integrity problems; a good paper nobody finds has no impact.

RTVACOS: where can your work break?

Research has an effect only if it is found, judged credible and reused. RTVACOS stands for seven criteria: rigour, transparency, visibility, adoption, collaboration, outreach and societal relevance. The first four are foundations: a weakness in one can undermine the whole. The last three are amplifiers: they strengthen a solid base but cannot replace it. The interactive tree lets you tick what your project already does and see where it is fragile.

Open the RTVACOS tree (interactive tool) Strengthen each foundation The talk, summary and references

Schematically, E ∝ (R × T × V × A) × (1 + C + O + S): E denotes the excellence being discussed, each letter one criterion, and the sign ∝ (“proportional to”) expresses a conceptual relationship, not a calibrated equation. RTVACOS is a framework for reflection, not a scoring instrument, and claims no empirical validation.

Influence, independently of size

Counting highly cited researchers mostly measures size: a large university has more of everything. The Influence Index, by Cyril Voyant, Jeroen Baas, Roy Boverhof, Paolo Stringari, Mathilde Marchand-Lasserre and John P. A. Ioannidis, looks at their share instead.

The terms. The Stanford–Elsevier Top 2 % lists gather the scientists in the top 2 % of their subfield according to a composite citation indicator (defined in the next section). For an institution, N is the number of eligible authors (those retained in the primary analysis of the dataset, also defined below), X the number of them in the lists, and p = X/N their share. The reference p₀ = 0.02 is the share expected by construction of the lists.

I=log2(pp0),p=XN,p0=0.02

Plain text: I = log2(p / p0), with p = X / N and p0 = 0.02; change over time: ΔI = I_cur − I_hist.

I is unitless and log₂ is the base-two logarithm, so each unit is a doubling: I = 0 is the reference, I = +1 twice it, I = −1 half of it. I is not defined when X = 0, and cannot exceed log₂(50) ≈ 5.6, reached when every eligible author is in the lists. The repository computes I twice: with the career-long lists (historical, I_hist) and with the lists of the most recent year (current, I_cur); ΔI > 0 means the concentration is increasing. As with comparing a forecast with persistence, the reference that copies the last known value, a score means something only against a stated reference.

Worked example (illustrative values). An institution has N = 500 eligible authors, 20 of them in the lists: p = 20/500 = 0.04 and I = log₂(0.04/0.02) = +1, twice the reference. With 5 of 500, p = 0.01 and I = −1.

French institutions: preliminary results

Submitted manuscript (Scientometrics); preliminary results. The repository publishes the processed data for 240 French institutions (Career.xlsx, career-long lists; Single.xlsx, most recent year). The figure shows the universities with at least 1,000 eligible authors.

How to read it:

  • Above the reference is the norm here. Eligible authors are senior, productive researchers: in the primary analysis of the RSOS dataset, 12 % of them are in the lists (145,704 of 1,214,702, section 3.1). In the file, all 137 French institutions with at least 100 eligible authors have I between 0.4 and 3.6. I > 0 means above the 2 % reference, not above other institutions.
  • Intervals before order. When two intervals overlap, the file does not separate the two institutions.
  • One indicator. It says nothing about teaching, innovation, integrity or the quality of a given piece of work.

Impact and integrity, side by side

The second piece of work, led by John P. A. Ioannidis with Jeroen Baas, Roy Boverhof and Cyril Voyant, asks whether impact can be read together with signals of research-integrity problems, institution by institution, across all of science (Royal Society Open Science 13, 260870, 2026).

Read the article (open access) Preprint (bioRxiv) Data (Mendeley Data)

What is counted (sections 2.1 to 2.6). Scopus, the bibliographic database, frozen on 1 August 2025: 10,933,183 authors with at least five full papers. A top-cited author is in the top 2 % of their subfield (174 subfields) or among the top 100,000 of all science, by a composite citation indicator that combines six indicators: total citations, the h-index, Schreiber's hm-index (adjusted for co-authorship), and citations to papers as single author, as single or first author, and as single, first or last author. The primary analysis keeps authors who started publishing in 1980 or later, with at least 40 Scopus-indexed items, at least five of them as single, first or last author. Three proxies (indirect indicators) of possible integrity problems are added: retractions not owing to publisher or journal error, self-citation above the 95th percentile of the subfield, and publication in titles discontinued by Scopus above the 95th percentile.

The score (section 2.7):

N(t)−[ΣR2+(S(t)−(0.05)N(t))+(D(t)−(0.05)N(t))]N(a)

Plain text: score = (N(t) − [ΣR/2 + (S(t) − (0.05)N(t)) + (D(t) − (0.05)N(t))]) / N(a)

  • N(t): top-cited authors of the institution in the primary analysis; N(a): all its authors in the primary analysis.
  • S(t): top-cited authors in the primary analysis with self-citations above the 95th percentile; D(t): those whose share of items in discontinued titles exceeds the 95th percentile.
  • ΣR: sum of retraction authorships (retractions not owing to publisher or journal error) of all institutional authors with at least five published items who started publishing in 1980 or later; a retracted paper with three authors from the institution counts three.

Without the bracket, the score is the share of top-cited authors, N(t)/N(a). Two retraction authorships cancel one top-cited author. S(t) and D(t) give a bonus or a penalty depending on whether they are below or above 5 % of N(t). The score has no fixed bounds: it becomes negative when the adjustments exceed N(t). The authors call these weights largely arbitrary and test them (section 2.8): thresholds p = 80, 95, 99 and retraction divisor f = 1, 2, 4, in plain text (N(t) − [ΣR/f + (S(t,p) − (1 − p/100)N(t)) + (D(t,p) − (1 − p/100)N(t))]) / N(a). The scores are then turned into percentiles among the 2,380 institutions with at least 100 primary-analysis authors.

Worked example (illustrative values). N(a) = 1,000 and N(t) = 100, so 10 % before adjustment. With ΣR = 10, S(t) = 8 and D(t) = 3: 100 − [10/2 + (8 − 5) + (3 − 5)] = 100 − 6 = 94, and 94/1,000 = 9.4 %.

Limits stated by the authors (section 4). The three proxies are warning signals, not proof of misconduct, and the summary score is a heuristic, not a validated ranking. Percentiles are relative to the 2,380 institutions and are not an absolute measure of institutional or national integrity. Retractions cover only part of flawed papers, some are honest errors, and each co-author receives the same penalty. Each author has a single affiliation, so small institutions are sensitive to small errors. Data and tables are open: anyone can redo the analysis with other weights.

Indicators to examine, not judgments

  • Brings: indicators that separate size from concentration; impact read together with integrity; open data and code; a reflection framework on rigour, transparency, visibility and adoption.
  • Limits: citation-based indicators are proxies, not judgments of individuals or of quality; integrity proxies flag patterns to examine, not misconduct. The Influence Index results for French institutions are preliminary (submitted manuscript). RTVACOS is a framework for reflection, not a scoring instrument.
