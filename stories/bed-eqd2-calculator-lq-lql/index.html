Free BED and EQD2 calculator: LQ or LQL?

LQL-Equiv is a free, open-source (MIT) calculator of biologically effective dose (BED) and of the equivalent dose in 2 Gy fractions (EQD2; Gy is the gray, the unit of absorbed dose) under the linear-quadratic (LQ) and linear-quadratic-linear (LQL) models. It runs in the browser, and nothing entered leaves the page.

Its authors are Cyril Voyant and Daniel Julian. Daniel Julian is a medical physicist and radiation protection adviser in the radiophysics team of the Centre de Cancérologie du Grand Montpellier (CCGM). He co-authored the first MATLAB version and its 2014 article, then this web version (LinkedIn).

It is a research and teaching tool, not a medical device: not for choosing or changing a prescription.

Cyril Voyant · Mines Paris – PSL, Centre O.I.E. · 2 November 2026

In brief

  • BED and EQD2 put different radiotherapy schedules on one scale; the model, LQ or LQL, must be stated.
  • For one 15 Gy fraction to the rectum, BED is 72.7 Gy under LQ and 59.4 Gy under LQL.
  • LQL-Equiv computes in the browser; it is a research and teaching tool, not a medical device.

Reading the quantities.

  • BED is biologically effective dose; EQD2 is the equivalent dose expressed in 2 Gy fractions.
  • LQ means linear-quadratic and LQL linear-quadratic-linear. n counts fractions, d is dose per fraction and Gy is the gray (energy deposited per unit mass).
  • The tissue parameter α/β (how sensitive the tissue is to the size of each fraction) and transition dose dT are expressed in Gy.
  • NTCP and TCP denote normal-tissue complication and tumour-control probabilities within the stated model.

LQ or LQL?

For n fractions of dose d, the linear-quadratic model gives BED = n · d · (1 + d / (α/β)), and EQD2 = BED / (1 + 2/(α/β)). The LQ curve keeps bending upward as the dose per fraction grows. The LQL model keeps it up to a transition dose dT, then continues in a straight line.

Below the transition dose, the two models agree. Above it, they part. For one fraction of 15 Gy to the rectum (α/β = 3.9 Gy, dT = 7.8 Gy):

  • LQ: 15 × (1 + 15/3.9) = 72.7 Gy;
  • LQL: 7.8 × (1 + 7.8/3.9) + 5 × (15 − 7.8) = 59.4 Gy, or 18 % lower (5 is the dimensionless slope of the linear part).

The model must therefore be stated whenever hypofractionated (fewer, larger fractions) and conventional schedules are compared.

Reading the formula

What the calculator covers

  • Documented parameters for 34 organs at risk and 20 tumour sites.
  • Treatment gaps, two fractions a day and tumour repopulation.
  • NTCP and TCP as model illustrations, never validated against clinical outcomes.
  • The 2026 version reproduces the 2014 software on 4,438 schedules: 69,125 of 69,131 values agree.

Two documented examples

The LQL-Equiv README gives two cases computed by the application, against the standard tumor of its library (proliferation of 0.66 Gy per day after 21 days; reference dose 2 Gy). These are its results, copied as published.

Other calculators

Other tools exist, such as the eviQ BED and EQD2 calculators. Check which model and which α/β each one uses before comparing results.

Limits

The result is only as good as the model and its parameters (α/β, dT, slope of the linear tail), which remain uncertain.
