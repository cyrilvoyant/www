The RTVACOS tree: where can your work break?

Research has an effect only if it is found, judged credible and reused. The RTVACOS framework (an acronym of seven criteria, spelled out just below) describes this as a multiplicative system: four foundations, where a single weakness drags everything down, and three amplifiers (collaboration, outreach, societal relevance), which scale a solid base but never replace it.

The framework equation

The preprint sums up the framework in a single relation (Equation 1, Section 3.3):

E∝(R × T × V × A)foundations×(1 + C + O + S)amplifiers

Plain text: E ∝ (R × T × V × A) × (1 + C + O + S)

E
research excellence, operationalized as impact capacity: the capacity of a piece of work to be found, trusted, reproduced and used by others.
∝
“is proportional to”. The authors chose this symbol over “=” on purpose: the equation does not produce a score to compare researchers or institutions; it is a heuristic (a rule of reasoning) that encodes a logical dependency.
R, T, V, A
the four foundations, first factor: Rigor, Transparency, Visibility, Adoption potential. Their product approaches zero as soon as any single term approaches zero.
C, O, S
the three amplifiers, second factor: Collaboration, Outreach, Societal relevance. They scale an already solid foundation but add little if it is weak. Thanks to the “1 +”, zero amplifiers leave the second factor at 1: only the foundations count.

Why a product? Because a single foundation at zero cancels impact, whatever the other terms. The preprint's example: a rigorous, well-cited paper whose code was never deposited and whose data are proprietary has zero transparency; nobody can build on it. The foundations form a chain (one broken link and the whole collapses), the amplifiers a lever that only works if the chain holds. A sum, by contrast, would let a strength compensate for a weakness. The authors kept the product for its simplicity: elementary operations, no weighting and no parameters to calibrate. Worked example (the preprint's Case C, scores from 0 to 3): E ∝ (2 × 2 × 2 × 2) × (1 + 2 + 1 + 1) = 16 × 5 = 80; with T = 0, the same calculation gives 0.

Try it. Give each dimension a score from 0 to 3, as in the preprint's illustrative rubric (Table 1), or load one of the three cases it analyzes (Appendix C).

A tool for reflection, not for ranking: the rubric is illustrative, each discipline must adapt it, and the authors themselves stress that the equation is not yet empirically validated. These numbers are not meant to compare researchers.

Tick what your project already does. Roots are foundations, branches are amplifiers. A bare root turns the whole tree pale: that is the multiplicative logic.
