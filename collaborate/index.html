Collaborate: research projects, studies and adaptation of the tools

Your question, a method you can check, a result you can defend.

These pages present the theory, the code, the limits and the conditions for reuse. The next step is your data and your decision. Bring a real problem; we start from what is known about the system, evaluate against an explicit reference, and add complexity only where it pays.

Three ways to work together

1

Answer a call for projects

National (ANR, the French National Research Agency; ADEME, the French Agency for Ecological Transition), European (Horizon Europe) or regional calls, as partner or coordinator: energy forecasting, renewables on the grid, evaluation of models, medical physics, responsible AI in research.

2

Fund it directly with the school

A collaborative research contract: a study, an internship, a co-supervised doctorate (for example a French CIFRE doctorate, carried out with a company) or a postdoctoral project.

3

Explore a research direction together

An academic collaboration to explore a research question together, compare approaches or open a new scientific direction. Each team’s objectives and contributions are defined from the outset, with a view to sharing or applying the work: a joint paper, open software, a dataset or transfer to practice.

What can be adapted to your data

  • Forecast evaluation: NICE metrics (errors divided by those of persistence, the forecast that repeats the last value), γ-index (a test that accepts small shifts) for forecast fields.
  • Reference forecasts: BLEND and CLIPER (mixes of persistence with the value one cycle earlier, or with the mean), ARTU (a reference weighing the last two values and the mean), variability and forecastability with sCV and F (variability due to clouds, and forecastability).
  • Frugal learning: MIMO-ELM (one frugal neural network, an ELM, forecasting several signals) for an energy mix, satellite irradiance forecasting.
  • Medical physics: LQL-Equiv (linear-quadratic-linear model) for dose equivalence, the radioiodine therapy model.
  • Simulation and AI in practice: road tunnel loads, WebSemantic (a language model reads, validated code computes).

How it works

  1. Write a short email: the question, the data, the decision it serves, the timeline.
  2. A first exchange frames the problem: what is known about the system, which reference to beat, what precision is really needed.
  3. Academic exploration develops directly between researchers. Depending on the project, a research contract or a service agreement (testing, targeted analysis) may be established. Where needed, the relevant departments at Mines Paris – PSL define the terms for intellectual property, confidentiality and publication.
  4. Once the framework is agreed, we define a clear research plan: objectives, methods, data, staffing and technical resources, milestones, schedule and deliverables. Evaluation criteria and decision points are set from the outset to track progress and make evidence-based adjustments. Results are shared under the terms of the agreement.
